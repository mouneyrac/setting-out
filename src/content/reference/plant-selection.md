The short answer: build every Perth plant list in the same order. Start in the
[Waterwise Plant Directory](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/waterwise-plants),
filter to **readily available** so you only specify what a nursery can actually
supply, then check any tree against the
[shot-hole borer host list](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/siteassets/documents/biosecurity/incidents/pshb/pshb-wa-host-list.pdf)
and any unfamiliar exotic against the
[Western Australian Organism List](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/online-tools/western-australian-organism-list/).
That is three checks and about ten minutes, and it is the difference between a
schedule that survives contact with a wholesaler and one that does not.

## The three directories, and what each is for

| Use | Tool | What it gives you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shortlisting | [Waterwise Plant Directory](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/waterwise-plants) | Search by suburb or name; filter by availability, origin, garden type, colour, growth habit |
| Local provenance | [Apace WA suburb selector](https://www.apacewa.org.au/resources/suburb-selector/) | What grew on your soil before the subdivision — 17 Perth vegetation complexes |
| Horticultural detail | [Kings Park grow-it-at-home](https://www.bgpa.wa.gov.au/grow-it-at-home) | Mature size, aspect, drainage, planting season, pruning, per species |

Greenlife Industry WA is the industry body behind the directory; its useful
contribution to a plant list is the
[fifteen accredited Waterwise Garden Centre locations](https://greenlifeindustrywa.com.au/waterwise-garden-centres/),
where staff have been trained on the range rather than merely stocking it.

One honest limitation. Every plant in the directory is already classed as
waterwise, so it will not rank plants by water need. Hydrozoning is still your job.

## Hydrozones

Water Corporation's [five-step method](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/garden/creating-a-waterwise-garden-in-5-steps)
makes step two grouping plants with similar water requirements together. The only
published, verified Perth dataset that scores individual species on water need is
the tree guide, which uses a three-point drop rating. Borrow it for the whole garden.

| Zone | Water Corporation drop rating | Meaning | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — dry | **0** | No supplementary watering once established | Verge, front garden, far boundary, hot west walls |
| 2 — low | **1** | Once a week once established | Main beds, screening, most of the garden |
| 3 — regular | **2** | Twice a week once established | Entry, courtyard, outdoor room — small, and near the house |

Zone 3 is capped by law, not by what a plant would like: Perth irrigation runs only
on rostered days inside a fixed window, and stops altogether from 1 June to 31
August. That is the argument for keeping zone 3 small and putting it where it is
seen. Rostered days,
the winter switch-off and the new-garden exemption are in
[the sprinkler roster reference](/reference/sprinkler-roster/); the physical
station-by-station design is in
[Irrigation 3](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydrozoning-and-stations/).

## Phosphorus-sensitive plants

Perth's sands hold almost nothing, so fertiliser either goes into the plant or
into the river. SERCUL is blunt: garden fertilisers are "one of the largest threats
to our rivers as nutrients added to our sandy soils are easily leached", and its
[lawn benchmark](https://www.sercul.org.au/our-projects/fertilise-wise/) is an
N:P:K of **10:1:6**.

The plants to watch are the Proteaceae. ANPSA names them directly — "many members
of the Protea family such as *Banksia*, *Grevillea*, *Telopea* and *Hakea*" — and
gives the two ratios you shop with:

| Situation | N:P:K | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Many Australian plants, general purpose | about 12:14:10 | [ANPSA](https://anpsa.org.au/frequently-asked-questions/) |
| Phosphorus-sensitive species | about 10:3:6 | [ANPSA](https://anpsa.org.au/frequently-asked-questions/) |
| Perth lawn | 10:1:6 | [SERCUL](https://www.sercul.org.au/our-projects/fertilise-wise/) |

Do not overstate it to a client. A sensitivity trial found "about 82% did not show
any of the symptoms that are typical of phosphorus toxicity", with *Banksia*,
*Hakea* and *Grevillea* among the *less* affected genera. The practical rule stands
anyway, because on this soil the low-phosphorus choice protects the Swan and Canning
regardless of what it does for the plant. Kings Park's own instruction for its
banksias is simply a native slow-release fertiliser at planting and annually.

## What varies by council, and how to get your answer

Verge species lists, height limits, whether a verge permit is needed, street tree
programs and subsidised plant giveaways are all set by individual local
governments; WALGA represents 139 of them. Water Corporation only goes as far as
saying every Gold
Waterwise Council has a verge policy or guideline. Find your client's council in
the [WALGA directory](https://www.walga.asn.au/about-local-government/online-local-government-directory.aspx),
search its site for "verge" and "street tree", and put the printed policy in the
job file. See also [Perth soils](/reference/perth-soils/) for what your dune system
will hold, and [the Noongar six seasons](/reference/noongar-seasons/) for when to
plant it.
