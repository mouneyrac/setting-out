Every phase produced something: a brief, a site analysis, a concept, a documentation
set, a cost plan, a build record, a defects list, a maintenance calendar, and the
review you just finished. Right now they are separate entries in a workbook.

This lesson makes them one document. That sounds like filing, and it is not. What
you assemble here is the record a buyer's building inspector, your insurer or the
ATO will ask about; the reference you will use when you do this again on the next
part of the block; and a portfolio piece that sits next to professional work without
embarrassment.

Most of the ninety minutes is assembly. The only real writing is one page.

## What a professional does at this stage

A landscape business closes a job by producing a project record, for commercial
reasons rather than sentimental ones. It is what they show the next client, what
they enter into awards, and what protects them when someone rings in three years
asking why the retaining wall drains the way it does. The industry has formalised
what goes in it. The
[AILA 2026 Awards entry guide](https://www.aila.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/_AILA/Awards/Entry%20Guide%202026.pdf)
asks for a 100-word project overview, a 700-word statement of achievement, a simple
site plan, five landscape-orientation hero images at a minimum of 300 dpi, and up to
fifteen supplementary images — all named `ProjectName-Image#-PhotographerName`. It
suggests the mix: two or three images of site context, two or three of different
elevations, two or three of design details, and two or three "before" images.

Notice two things. The written component is short — 800 words for a project that
took a year. And "before" images are treated as a normal part of a serious
submission, which is the advantage a renovator has and a new build never does.

Build to that shape.

If you have used the workbook on this site, [/my/workbook](/my/workbook) prints the
whole thing or downloads it as one Markdown file, which gives you the text spine in
a single step. Everything else is drawings, photographs and PDFs in folders.

What people always find missing is the buried services. If your as-built drawing
does not fix the mainline from two permanent points, measure it now, before the lawn
closes over.

## The one-page summary

Write this last, once everything else is stacked up. One page, no more. It is the
only part most people will ever read, and the part that will still make sense to you
in ten years.

- **The property.** Address, orientation, block and garden area, soil type, depth to
  limestone or clay if you hit it.
- **The brief in one sentence.** What you asked this garden to do.
- **The three constraints that shaped it.** The three that actually drove decisions,
  not all of them.
- **What was built,** with quantities. 62 m² of paving, 14 lineal metres of limestone
  retaining to 600 mm, six irrigation stations, 41 plants.
- **Who did what.** Which parts you did, and which licensed trades did the rest —
  electrical, plumbing, bore, retaining above the exempt height. Name them.
- **Dates and money.** Start, finish, final cost against budget, and the honest
  reason for the variance.
- **Three things that worked and three you would change.** Specific ones. "Should
  have run the conduit before we paved" is worth a page of reflection.

If it will not fit on the page, you are writing a diary rather than a summary.

## Photographs

Pair them. A before shot and an after shot from the same position, at the same
height, in the same light, is worth more than twenty attractive photographs of the
finished garden. If you took the "before" set during your
[existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/), go
and stand in exactly those spots again, and mark the positions on your site plan
with an arrow so the set you take in five years matches too.

Name every file as you file it. `Backyard-01-Before-NorthEast.jpg` beats
`IMG_4471.jpg` and costs the same effort once.
[Photographing your project](/curriculum/handover/photographing-your-project/)
covers light, height and timing.

Two cautions. A photograph is dated evidence: if a pool gate in frame is not
compliant, the record now contains that — fix the thing rather than cropping the
photo. And before you share anything publicly, check the background for alarm
panels, meter boxes, gate codes and your neighbour's windows.

## Formats, naming and storage

The National Archives publishes the list so you do not have to guess. For an archive
you may not open for a decade, use
[the formats they nominate](https://www.naa.gov.au/information-management/storing-and-preserving-information/preserving-information/born-digital-file-format-standards):
PDF for documents, TIFF or PNG or JPEG for images, CSV for anything tabular, SVG for
vector drawings.

Then apply the [3-2-1 rule](https://www.naa.gov.au/explore-collection/search-people/researching-your-family/caring-your-personal-digital-collection/storing-your-digital-archive):
three copies, on two different types of storage, one of them somewhere else. The
Archives states plainly that a USB flash drive is not long-term storage. Computer,
plus an external drive, plus cloud satisfies it. When you send a copy to someone,
copy the file and convert the copy — never edit the original.

## The copy that lives with the property records

Put the whole thing with the certificate of title, the settlement papers and the
house insurance. Three concrete reasons.

**Tax.** The ATO expects [records of buying, owning and selling a property —
including capital expenditure on improvements — to be kept for at least five years
after you dispose of it](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/property-and-capital-gains-tax/keeping-records-for-property). A main residence is generally exempt from capital gains
tax, but the ATO's own advice is to keep the records anyway in case that stops being
true, which it does the moment you rent out part of the house.

**Sale.** A buyer's building inspector will ask about the retaining wall, the pool
barrier and anything that looks like it needed a permit. Without the paperwork the
fallback is a council archive search — at the City of Vincent,
[$80 for a residential search of records from 1994 onward, about 15 working days,
non-refundable whether or not anything is found](https://www.vincent.wa.gov.au/plan-build/build/regulations-information/request-plans.aspx),
and it only ever returns approved records. Unapproved work is a separate and dearer
conversation, covered in
[what landscaping does to a property value](/curriculum/review/landscaping-and-property-value/).

**Insurance.** After a storm puts a tree through the pergola, the claim is settled on
what you can evidence. Invoices, quantities and dated photographs are that evidence.

## Score yourself against the trade

Last job, fifteen minutes. Open the
[LIAWA 2026 residential judging criteria](https://www.landscaping.net.au/static/uploads/files/2026-liawa-awards-judging-criteria-wfixkaijxyiy.pdf)
and score your own garden 1 to 5 on each of the seven criteria WA's industry body
uses: design excellence, sustainability, quality of workmanship, plant selection and
health, maintenance and longevity, value to client, and professionalism. The
residential categories are banded by project value — under $10,000, $10,000 to
$30,000, over $30,000 — so compare yourself against work of a similar spend rather
than against a magazine.

Write the scores onto the summary page with one line of justification each. The
lowest score is the curriculum for your next project.

## Where this leaves you

That is the end of the process, and the point of having gone through it in order.
You hold the same document set a landscape business would hold on a job this size,
on your own block, for a garden you understand from the sand up.

Two things carry forward. The calendar from
[the Perth year](/curriculum/maintenance/the-perth-year/) is live now and runs
whether or not you look at it. And this portfolio is where the next project starts,
because the second job on a block is always a variation on the first — and you no
longer have to guess where the mainline runs.
