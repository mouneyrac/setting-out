You have been looking at this garden every day for months, which is exactly why
you can no longer see it. Somewhere on the block sits a paver 8 mm proud of its
neighbour, a sprinkler misting onto the fence, and a light aimed into next
door's bedroom. You have walked past all three about forty times.

The defect walkthrough is the deliberately unsentimental hour that finds them.
It is not a celebration lap — you get that later, with the camera, in
[photographing your project](/curriculum/handover/photographing-your-project/).

One rule makes it work: every item gets written down, and every written item
gets a fix and a date. Nothing leaves the list because you decided it was
probably fine. Items leave the list when they are done.

## What a professional does at this stage

On a job run properly, the contractor writes the defect list before the client
does. That is economics, not politeness. A defect you find yourself is a
half-hour fix with the tools still on the trailer. The same defect found by the
client three weeks later is a return visit and an argument about whether it was
always like that.

The second reason matters more on your own block: **the list is what lets the
job end.** Without a written, closed-out list a renovation does not finish, it
gradually stops. Six months later there is still a bag of joint sand in the
shed and a station that has never worked, and the project quietly reclassifies
itself in your head as "not quite right".

So: print your plan, number the zones on it, and walk them in that order.
Wandering is how you inspect the bits you are proud of four times and the side
access never. Carry a 1.2 m level and a 2 m straightedge, a tape, a torch, a
hose with a trigger nozzle, chalk or survey flags to mark each defect where it
stands, and your plan and plant schedule — you are checking against what was
specified, not what you now remember specifying. Photograph every item as you
log it.

Walk once mid-morning and once after dark. Lighting defects are invisible in
daylight and paving lippage is invisible at night.

## The zone-by-zone walk

### Hard surfaces

- **Trip hazards first.** Any lip between adjacent units, or between paving and
  a threshold, that you can feel through a shoe. Run the straightedge across
  joints and doorways. Check step risers are consistent — an odd riser in a
  flight is the classic ankle-breaker.
- **Falls away from the house.** This is where renovations quietly go wrong,
  because you have raised levels against a building set out for the old ones.
  NCC clause 3.3.3 wants 50 mm of fall over the first metre away from the slab
  (25 mm in low rainfall intensity areas over an impermeable surface), and the
  slab kept clear of the ground beside it — 50 mm above adjacent impermeable
  paving, 150 mm above finished ground in the general case. If you have buried
  a weephole or brought paving up level with the slab, that is a defect, not a
  detail.
- **Ponding.** Hose-test it (see the table below). Chalk the puddle edge while
  the water is still there.
- **Workmanship.** Cut pavers narrower than about a third of a full unit,
  visible saw marks, chipped arrises, joints not topped up, edge restraint
  missing at the perimeter. Efflorescence is cosmetic and weathers off — log it,
  do not chase it.
- **Poured surfaces.** Crazing in liquid limestone or concrete is cosmetic. A
  crack with a level change across it, or a slab that rocks, is not.

Any of these that needs a paver re-cut means dry-cutting masonry, and silica
dust is the real hazard there, not the noise: wet cut, on-tool extraction, and
a properly fitted P2. See
[site establishment and safety](/curriculum/build/site-establishment-and-safety/).

### Walls, steps and structures

Sight along the top of every wall from one end — your eye finds a hump better
than any level does. Check the aggie outlet is visible and discharging, capping
is fixed rather than just sitting there, post fixings and bolts are tight, and
nothing sharp or protruding sits at shin height.

If any of it is near a pool fence, stop and think about the non-climbable zone
before you write "complete". A new bench, pot, raised bed or step beside a
compliant barrier can make it non-compliant, and your local government
re-inspects at least every four years.

### Drainage

Soakwell lids at grade and out of wheel tracks. Downpipes connected and sealed
with no gap at the top. Water surfacing around a lid during the hose test, or
pooling at the wall, means the connection or the soakwell is wrong now and will
be far worse in a June storm. Cross-check against
[drainage and soakwells](/curriculum/build/drainage-and-soakwells/).

### Irrigation

You cannot just switch it on for an hour. In Perth you may run **each station
for no more than two minutes for testing**, on your rostered watering days,
before 9am or after 6pm, standing in the garden while it runs. Between **1 June
and 31 August** the Sprinkler Switch-off applies to scheme and bore water alike
and you cannot legally test your own system at all — a Waterwise Garden
Irrigator can, being exempt from the schedule. Handing over in winter is a real
scenario; book the irrigator rather than risk a $100 fine.

Per station, standing in the zone: every head pops and retracts, nothing buried
by mulch or turf, no misting (over-pressure), no dribbling (under-pressure), no
overspray onto paving, fences or the neighbour, and the valve actually shuts
off. Then catch-cup your two largest zones — containers spread evenly, at least
a metre from the nearest sprinkler. If the driest holds less than half the
average, that zone cannot be scheduled sensibly.
[Irrigation install and audit](/curriculum/build/irrigation-install-and-audit/)
has the full method.

### Lighting

Come back after full dark, not at dusk. Sit in the seating position and look
for glare, then walk to the street and to the neighbour's boundary and look
back. Every fitting on, none flickering, no condensation inside a lens,
transformers and drivers mounted clear of the ground, and no dark step or level
change the lighting has missed.

Anything at 240 V outdoors is licensed work in WA — it is illegal for an
unlicensed person to modify fixed wiring, and your electrical contractor owes
you an **electrical safety certificate within 28 days**. Chase it now, while
you still have their number in your phone. See
[garden lighting](/curriculum/build/garden-lighting/).

### Planting, soil and finishes

Check plants against the schedule, not against memory: right species, right
count, right size, substitutions recorded. Then that each sits at grade rather
than proud or buried, mulch is pulled clear of stems, stakes and ties are not
girdling, mulch is a consistent 50–75 mm and off the paving, turf joints are
tight and level with adjacent surfaces, and edges are cut clean.

Look hard along every trench line you backfilled. Settlement shows there first,
and topping up now is far cheaper than after the turf has knitted.

## Test every system, on a pass or fail

| System | Test | Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface falls at the house | Straightedge and level | 50 mm fall over the first metre; slab clear of adjacent paving |
| Paving drainage | Hose 10 min, inspect after 15 | No standing water anywhere |
| Downpipes and soakwells | Hose down each downpipe, 5 min | Nothing surfacing at lids or at the wall |
| Irrigation, per station | 2 min on a rostered day | Every head pops, retracts, no misting or overspray |
| Irrigation uniformity | Catch cups, two largest zones | Driest cup at least half the average |
| Controller | Read the programme back | Matches the schedule you wrote, correct start times, rain sensor live |
| Lighting | Full dark | No glare from seat, street or boundary; no dark trip hazard |
| Gates and latches | 20 open-and-close cycles | Latches every time, from every approach angle |

### The zone nobody walks: paperwork

Add it to the list with a date like everything else.

- **BA7 notice of completion.** For anything built under a building permit —
  patio, pergola, retaining wall, pool — the responsible person must lodge it
  with the local government within seven days of completion. Your six-year
  window to complain to the Building Commissioner starts on the date that form
  was lodged, so "they never got around to it" is your problem too.
- **Electrical safety certificate** within 28 days.
- **Pool barrier.** Ring your local government to arrange the initial
  inspection after handover.
- Receipts, warranties and compliance certificates go into
  [warranties and records](/curriculum/handover/warranties-and-records/).

## Get someone else to walk it

You will defend your own decisions. That is not a character flaw, it is how
anyone behaves about work they did themselves — which is why one pair of eyes
is never enough on your own job.

Pick someone who was not involved, ideally someone who will use the garden
differently to you: a kid, someone unsteady on their feet, someone tall enough
to hit the pergola beam. Do not walk around narrating. Hand them a phone and
twenty minutes alone, then ask three specific questions instead of "what do you
think":

1. Where would you not walk at night?
2. What looks unfinished?
3. What is going to annoy you in a year?

Vague questions get vague answers. Those three reliably produce items.

## When the defect is not yours to fix

If a trade did the work, make your own list first, then walk it with them.
Under the Australian Consumer Law the work must be done with acceptable care
and skill. Put each item in writing with a photograph, the specific fix and a
reasonable date, and keep the email.

If they will not come back, the Building Commissioner considers complaints on
home building work contracts valued between $7,500 and $500,000, generally
within six years of completion — but only three years for contract disputes
such as variations and payments. Note also that from
[1 July 2026](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home)
Class 10a work — patios, pergolas, carports, sheds — under $50,000 no longer
requires a registered building contractor, so check what you actually engaged
before you assume which regulator applies.

## What happens to this next

Do not close the list. It is live until every date has passed and every line is
struck out.

While you are still on site with the plan in your hand, the next lesson records
what was actually built — mainline runs, cable routes, valve positions,
soakwell depths — as as-built drawings. That is the one record that stops a
future you digging up a new lawn to find a solenoid.
