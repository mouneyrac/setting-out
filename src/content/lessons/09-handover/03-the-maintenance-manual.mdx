A landscape contractor's handover pack is three documents: the as-built drawings,
the warranty and receipts file, and the maintenance manual. The first two protect
you legally. The third decides whether the garden is still alive in eighteen
months.

It matters more on a renovation than on a new build, because your block now holds
two populations with different needs — plants that have been in that sand for
fifteen years, and plants that went in last month. They cannot share a watering
schedule, and nobody can tell them apart from the front gate. The manual is where
you write down which is which.

The test for this document is blunt. Hand it to someone who has never seen your
garden, tell them you are away for three weeks in February, and see whether they
could keep it alive without ringing you.

## What a professional hands over, and why

A commercial handover manual exists to move risk. If a plant dies in month four
and the client's records show the schedule was followed, that is a warranty claim
against the contractor. If there is no manual, or the manual was never followed,
it is not. That is why the good ones are specific to the point of tedium.

You are both parties here, which changes the purpose but not the content. The
manual exists so nine phases of decisions survive a busy February, a house-sitter,
and yourself in four years having forgotten why station 3 runs longer than
station 2.

Six parts. Write them in this order, because the first one is time-critical.

| Part | Answers | Fails when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Establishment schedule | What to do for the first twelve weeks | Skipped, and the whole plant budget dies in its first summer |
| Irrigation programme | Exactly what the controller runs, per season | Written in minutes with no station map, so nobody dares change it |
| Plant-by-plant notes | What each plant needs and what stress looks like | Latin names only, no location, no photo |
| Fertiliser programme | What, how much, which months | Vague — "feed in spring" — so it gets over-applied or skipped |
| Pruning times | What gets cut, when, and what nobody touches | No distinction between a hedge trim and licensed work |
| Twelve-month calendar | The one page that gets looked at | Buried behind the other five |

## Part 1 — the first twelve weeks

Write this first and put it at the front, because it expires. New plantings need
frequent shallow watering while the root ball is still the only thing holding
water; established plants need the opposite.

In Perth this collides with the sprinkler roster, and the way through is a
[watering exemption](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/exemptions-and-breaches/watering-exemption/apply-for-a-watering-exemption).
Apply on the day the garden goes in. Planted between 1 October and 31 March you
get up to 42 days at 10 mm a day; between 1 April and 30 September, up to 35 days
at 7.5 mm a day. You are billed for the extra water, inspections happen, and
breaching the conditions risks a $500 fine. Write the exemption's end date in the
manual in bold, because that is the day the controller has to change and nothing
will remind you.

A workable twelve-week structure for a spring or autumn planting:

| Period | New plants | New turf | Retained plants |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Daily under exemption; check the root ball itself, not the surrounding sand | Daily, light and frequent, until knitted | Nothing new — normal rostered watering |
| Weeks 3–6 | Every second day, longer runs | Reduce to every second day, mow when it holds against a gentle tug | Watch for stress from disturbed roots |
| Weeks 7–12 | Back onto the rostered two days, hand-water any individual struggler | Rostered two days | Normal |
| Week 12 | Replace failures now, in season, not next summer | First light feed | Reassess anything that has declined since the works |

Two rules for whoever is holding the manual. Hand watering is allowed any day of
the week, preferably before 9am, with a watering can or a handheld hose with one
outlet and a nozzle — never left unattended. And keep mulch pulled back off stems
and trunks; collar rot from mulch piled against a stem is the most common
self-inflicted loss in a new Perth garden.

Full detail in [the first twelve weeks](/curriculum/maintenance/the-first-twelve-weeks/).

## Part 2 — the irrigation programme, season by season

This is the part house-sitters actually use, and the part most home manuals get
wrong by writing minutes with no explanation.

Start with the legal frame, in the manual, in plain words: two rostered days a
week allocated by street number, once a day, before 9am or after 6pm; the
[Sprinkler Switch-off](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/sprinkler-switch-off)
from 1 June to 31 August with a $100 fine; bore users on the same roster. Then
one line that saves a fine: **if a station comes on at the wrong time, turn the
controller to OFF, do not try to reprogram it in a hurry.**

Then a station table. One row per station, filled in from your
[hydrozoning](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydrozoning-and-stations/) and your
measured run times:

| Station | Waters | Head type | Measured rate | Minutes for 10 mm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Front lawn | Gear drive rotary | 15 mm/h | 40 |
| 2 | Front beds | Dripline | 18 mm/h | 33 |
| 3 | Rear natives (established) | Dripline | 18 mm/h | 20 |

Measure your own rates with catch cups rather than copying the published bands —
[precipitation rate and uniformity](/curriculum/build/irrigation-scheduling-and-uniformity/)
covers the method. Remember you may run each station for only two minutes at a
time for testing, on rostered days, inside the windows.

Then the seasonal layer. Do not write twelve separate programmes. Write one
summer programme and use the controller's seasonal adjust, per Water
Corporation's published Perth and South West figures:

| Month | Seasonal adjust | What that means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| January–February | 100% | The programme as written |
| March | 80% | Reduce on the first weekend of the month |
| April | 60% | |
| May | Manual only | Run it only if a dry spell demands it |
| June–August | Switch-off | Controller off at the wall. Hand watering only |
| September | Manual only | Roster resumes 1 September; still mostly rain-fed |
| October | 60% | Set the programme back up |
| November | 80% | |
| December | 90% | |

Finish the section with the controller facts page: brand and model, where the
manual lives, where the master valve and each solenoid box are, whether there is
a rain sensor, and what to do after a power failure. Photograph the programme
screen and print the photo. See
[controllers and compliance](/curriculum/build/irrigation-controllers-and-compliance/).

## Part 3 — plant-by-plant notes

One row per plant or group, ordered by where they are, not alphabetically. A
house-sitter navigates by location.

Columns worth having: location, common name, botanical name, water rating
(the [Waterwise Plants directory](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/waterwise-plants)
gives minimum, little or medium), station number, feed, prune when, what stress
looks like, and one photo.

On a renovation, add a status column: **new**, **retained** or **relocated**.
Retained plants carry the verdict and reason you recorded in the
[existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/) —
that is where a note like "olive kept for screening, expect suckering, remove
suckers each winter" belongs. Pull the rest from your
[planting plan and schedule](/curriculum/documentation/planting-plan-and-schedule/).

## Part 4 — the fertiliser programme

Perth sand holds almost no nutrient, so the failure mode is not under-feeding —
it is dumping fertiliser that leaches straight past the roots into the
groundwater and the Swan-Canning.

- **Lawn.** SERCUL's [Fertilise Wise](https://www.sercul.org.au/our-projects/fertilise-wise/)
  guidance: a complete fertiliser at N:P:K around 10:1:6, a maximum single
  application of 25 g/m² for couch and 12 g/m² for kikuyu and buffalo, applied
  only in September, October, November, March and April — never in summer or
  winter — and only when the lawn shows a deficiency such as yellowing.
- **Beds.** Slow-release, low-phosphorus, river-safe, with trace elements.
- **WA natives.** Banksias, hakeas, grevilleas and their Proteaceae relatives
  evolved on soils with almost no phosphorus and are damaged by ordinary garden
  fertiliser. Use a native-specific low-phosphorus product, or nothing.
- **Always.** Water in after applying. Never fertilise during a heatwave. Liquid
  feeds and seaweed only during active growth.

More in [fertilising Perth sand](/curriculum/maintenance/fertilising-perth-sand/).

## Part 5 — pruning times, and what nobody touches

Give each plant group a month, not a season, and state the reason — hedges on a
growth-flush cycle, natives after flowering, fruit trees in winter, ornamental
grasses cut back in late winter before new growth.

Then a page headed **do not attempt**. Say it plainly, because a willing
house-sitter is exactly the person who will have a go:

- **Anything near the overhead service to the house.** Western Power records
  [30-plus accidental contacts with the network every month](https://www.westernpower.com.au/safety/working-near-our-network/).
  Ring them for the approach distance; do not judge it from the ground.
- **Anything requiring a ladder or a chainsaw above shoulder height.** That is
  arborist work.
- **Any fault in garden lighting, a pump or a pool circuit.** Electrical work in
  WA is licensed work — see [garden lighting](/curriculum/build/garden-lighting/).
- **Anything that puts a climbable object beside the pool fence.** A pot, a bin
  or a stack of pruning stood against a barrier breaks compliance, and councils
  re-inspect at least every four years.

Detail in [pruning](/curriculum/maintenance/pruning/).

## Part 6 — the twelve-month calendar, and making it usable

One page, twelve rows, four columns: water, feed, prune, check. Put the fixed
dates on it — 1 June controller off, 1 September roster resumes, wetting agent at
the start of winter and again in early summer, mulch topped up to 5–10 cm
annually, retic service booked before October. Perth's rhythm fits the six Noongar
seasons better than four European ones; that framing is in
[the Perth year](/curriculum/maintenance/the-perth-year/).

Then make it survivable. Laminate the calendar and the station table and hang them
inside the shed or the meter box. Add an emergency page: who to ring for the
retic, the licensed electrician and Water Corporation faults. Photos beat plant
names for anyone who is not a gardener.

## What happens next

The manual is one of three handover documents. The
[as-built drawings](/curriculum/handover/as-built-drawings/) tell whoever digs
here next where the mainline and cables actually run. The next lesson builds the
third — [warranties and records](/curriculum/handover/warranties-and-records/) —
the file of receipts, plant guarantees, certificates and product data sheets that
turns this manual from a set of good intentions into something you can enforce.
