Most people go about this backwards. They collect images, fall in love with a
layout, get it drawn, and only then find out it costs three times what they were
prepared to spend. Then they cut the wrong things — the drainage, the soil, the
retic — because those are the invisible parts, and the garden fails four years
later for reasons that have nothing to do with the design.

A professional has the money conversation at the brief stage, before a line is
drawn, because the number changes the design. It is not an awkward preamble to
the real work. It is an input.

You are renovating, which means your budget carries a cost a display-home
landscape budget does not: getting rid of what is already there. Ripping out
tired retic, breaking up a cracked slab, carting away spoil and paying tip fees
all happen before a single new thing appears. Budget for it as a real line, not
a rounding error.

## What a professional actually does at this stage

A good designer does not ask "what's your budget?" and wait for you to guess.
They give you bands and watch which one makes you flinch. That is faster,
kinder, and it produces a real number rather than the number you think you are
supposed to say.

They also do three other things:

1. **Price the ground before the finishes.** Excavation, drainage, retaining and
   soil come out of the same pot as the paving, and on a Perth block with
   limestone at 400 mm or a two-metre fall to the back fence they can eat half
   of it. Your findings from [Perth soils](/curriculum/site-analysis/perth-soils/)
   and the levels survey belong in this conversation.
2. **State the three-way trade out loud.** Area, quality and time. You can fix
   two of them; the third moves. A client who wants the whole 200 square metres,
   in natural stone, finished by Christmas, is going to be disappointed by one
   of those three, and it is better to choose which now.
3. **Name what is being excluded.** Most quotes that look cheap are cheap
   because something is missing — drainage, soil preparation, disposal, the
   plumber's cut-in, the electrician. A professional says so.

If a designer will not discuss money before drawing, they are going to waste
yours.

| Source | What it gives you | What it costs |

| Three written quotes on **your** scope | The only number that binds anybody | Your time |
| Supplier price lists (Midland Brick, turf farms, soil yards) | Real supply-only material cost, today | Free |
| [RLB Riders Digest 2026 — Perth](https://www.rlb.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/1/2026/01/2026-RLB-Rider-Digest_Perth_Digital_2.pdf) | Dated Perth rates from a quantity surveyor with nothing to sell | Free |
| Rawlinsons Construction Cost Guide | 6,337 cost lines, trade-grade detail | $450 |
| Cost-guide websites | Directional bands only | Free |

If a range in this lesson matters to your decision, do not take it from here —
go and build the number. Ring three Perth suppliers for the material, add a
labour rate, and compare that against the quotes you get. That method survives
inflation; a number in an article does not.

## Perth budget bands

These are bands for a renovation on an established suburban block, based on the
sources above and dated to August 2026.

| Band | What it realistically buys |
| --- | --- |
| **Under $5,000** | DIY. Soil improvement, mulch, plants, retic repairs, turf to a modest area. Real and worthwhile — this is where the highest return per dollar in a Perth garden actually sits. |
| **$5,000 – $15,000** | One zone done properly by a contractor, or whole-yard soft landscaping: demolition, soil, retic overhaul, turf, planting, mulch and edging. |
| **$15,000 – $40,000** | The typical Perth backyard makeover. Demolition and disposal, retic, a paved courtyard or alfresco area, turf, garden beds, planting, edging, basic lighting. |
| **$40,000 – $80,000** | The above plus one or two big-ticket items — retaining, a deck, a pergola, substantial level changes, or pool surrounds. |
| **$80,000+** | Full design-and-construct with significant structures, extensive hard landscaping, or a pool in the scope. |

For comparison, the August 2026 Perth figures on
[What's The Damage](https://whatsthedamage.com.au/landscaping-cost-perth/) put a
basic backyard makeover at $5,250–$21,000 and a full landscape at
$15,750–$63,000, with landscaper hourly rates of $58–$115.

## Why the same paving job varies by a factor of three

Eight things, roughly in order of how much money they move:

- **What is already there.** Removing an existing slab or old paving, and
  carting it away, before you lay anything.
- **Access.** Machine access down the side means a bobcat. No access means
  wheelbarrows, and wheelbarrows are days.
- **Base preparation.** Deep sand needs compacted road base. Shallow sand over
  limestone may need the limestone broken out.
- **Falls and drainage.** Getting water away from the house properly costs more
  than getting it roughly away.
- **Cutting.** Curves, diagonals and lots of edges mean saw time and waste.
- **Edge restraint.** The thing people delete to save money, and the reason
  Perth paving spreads and drops at the edges within five years.
- **Quantity.** Small jobs carry a mobilisation cost. Forty square metres will
  not cost half of eighty.
- **Material.** The part everyone focuses on, and often the smallest variable.

## Contingency, and the rules that protect your money

Hold back **10% minimum** on a renovation, and **15–20%** if you have not opened
the ground yet, the block slopes, or you do not know where the services run. On
a renovation the surprises are underground and they are somebody else's — old
soakwells, buried rubble, a retic mainline nobody documented.

Quotes also go stale. Perth tender prices rose 5.4% in 2025 and are forecast to
rise 5.3% in 2026 (RLB Riders Digest, current as at 31 December 2025). A quote
you sit on for six months is not the quote you were given.

Three WA-specific things worth knowing before you hand over money. Landscaping
is explicitly covered by the Home Building Contracts Act 1991, so a fixed-price
job between $7,500 and $500,000 must be in a written, signed, dated contract.
The deposit is capped at 6.5% of the total. And home indemnity insurance —
which most people assume covers them — does **not** apply to landscaping done on
its own. If your landscaper disappears, that safety net is not there. The
detail is in the regulations above and is covered properly in
[contracts and variations](/curriculum/costing/contracts-and-variations/).

## Setting your number

Write down two figures: the total you are willing to spend, and the contingency
percentage sitting inside it. Then decide whether that is one project or three.
Staging is not a defeat — a staged Perth garden usually ends up better, because
you get to watch how the first stage behaves through a summer before committing
to the second. [Staging the project](/curriculum/costing/staging-the-project/)
covers how to split it without paying for the same mobilisation twice.
