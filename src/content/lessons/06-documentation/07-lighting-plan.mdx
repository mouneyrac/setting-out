Lighting is the sheet most people skip, and it has the least forgiving deadline
in the whole set. The cable goes in the ground during earthworks — before the
base, the paving, the soil and the mulch. If this drawing does not exist by then,
your later options are a saw cut through finished liquid limestone or an
extension lead across the lawn forever.

It is also the only drawing in your set with a criminal offence attached to
getting it wrong, so start there.

## The line this drawing has to make explicit

Regulation 4A of the [Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/RedirectURL?OpenAgent=&query=mrdoc_46377.htm)
defines **electrical work** as work on installations, appliances or equipment
supplied at a nominal pressure **exceeding 50 volts a.c. or 120 volts ripple free
d.c.** Regulation 19 makes carrying out electrical work an offence unless it is
authorised by a licence or permit, and
[Building and Energy](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/electrical-safety-home/having-electrical-work-done)
puts it plainly: all electrical work must be done by a person holding the
relevant licence. That voltage figure cuts your garden in two, and your plan
should show the cut.

| On the plan | Who may do it in WA |
| --- | --- |
| A new fixed weatherproof outdoor GPO for the transformer | Licensed electrical contractor |
| A hard-wired transformer, or any 230 V bollard, floodlight, wall light or pergola downlight | Licensed electrical contractor |
| Anything at the switchboard — new circuit, RCD, timer | Licensed electrical contractor |
| Bonding metal within 1.25 m of pool water, including light fittings | Licensed electrical contractor |
| A 12 V transformer plugged into an existing outdoor GPO | You |
| Cable, connectors and fixtures downstream of that transformer | You |

Mark every mains item with the same symbol — a circled **EC** works — and list
them in one block on the sheet. That block is the electrician's scope, and
booking it once at documentation stage is the difference between one call-out and
three. Contractors are **EC**, electricians are **EW**, both checkable on the
[WA Online Licence Search](https://ols.demirs.wa.gov.au/search), and for any
electrical work other than maintenance or like-for-like replacement your
contractor **must give you an electrical safety certificate within 28 days**.

## What a professional does at this stage

They draw lighting as two overlays, not one: a **fixture layout** (what is lit,
from where, aimed how) and a **cable and circuit plan** (transformer, runs,
sleeves, groupings). The first is a design decision. The second is a trenching
instruction, and it has to be finished earlier.

- **Fix the transformer and its GPO first.** It is the only part needing a
  licensed trade, and every cable length on the sheet is measured from it. Put it
  where you can read the label — sheltered, 300 mm or more above ground, out of
  sprinkler throw, not behind the hedge you are about to plant.
- **Coordinate sleeves in the same pass as the retic.** Every hard-surface
  crossing on the [irrigation plan](/curriculum/documentation/irrigation-plan/)
  gets a second, separate conduit for lighting cable, both shown on the
  [layout plan](/curriculum/documentation/scaled-layout-plan/) with an offset
  dimension from a permanent edge.
- **Design the dark as deliberately as the light.** Contrast is the entire
  effect; a car park is what you get when you remove it.
- **Specify, don't sketch.** A circle labelled "light" cannot be ordered, priced
  or aimed.

## Function first: the order you light things in

Work down this list and stop when the garden works. Features come last and they
come sparingly.

1. **Level changes.** Every step, ramp, the top and bottom of a retaining wall,
   the edge of a pool surround. This is the safety layer and the reason the
   drawing exists. Light the **tread**, not the person's eyes — from a riser
   light, a low sidewall fitting or a downlight above.
2. **Thresholds.** Back door, front door, gate latches, the bin run, the tap, the
   meter box. Anywhere someone stops, reaches or fumbles for a key.
3. **Circulation.** The routes people actually take after dark, from
   [circulation and sightlines](/curriculum/concept-design/circulation-and-sightlines/)
   — not the routes the plan wishes they took.
4. **Task zones.** The barbecue bench, the outdoor kitchen, the table. Enough to
   cook safely, separately switched so it is not on all evening.
5. **Features.** One or two. Three feature effects in a suburban back garden is
   already too many.

| Function | Technique | Typical fixture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steps and level changes | Downlight from above, or low sidelight across the tread | Recessed riser or step light, shielded |
| Path and lawn edge | Low pools of light with dark between, staggered | 250–450 mm path light with a solid top cowl |
| Threshold and door | Wall-mounted, aimed down | Shielded wall light (usually mains — mark EC) |
| Specimen tree | Up-lighting from two sides, fitting hidden in planting | Spike-mounted spotlight, narrow beam |
| Limestone or rendered wall | Grazing from close to the base to pick up texture | Spotlight or in-ground uplight, wide beam |
| Tree or sculpture against a lit wall | Silhouetting — fixture behind the object | Spotlight aimed at the wall, not the object |

Two Perth corrections. **Limestone bounces** — limestone walls, liquid limestone
and pale paving need noticeably less output than a catalogue photo of a dark
brick house suggests. And **in-ground uplights silt up here**: sand, mulch and
leaf litter fill the recess, then the fitting overheats or floods. Spike-mount
and hide the fixture in planting wherever you can, and reserve in-ground fittings
for paving where there is no alternative.

## Colour temperature, output and beam

Three specifications, one line each on your schedule.

- **Colour temperature: 2700 K, 3000 K at most, and the same figure everywhere.**
  Mixed colour temperatures are the clearest sign of a scheme that grew by
  accident. Warmer light also suits Perth limestone, brick and dry foliage,
  attracts fewer insects, and is what
  [Astrotourism WA](https://astrotourismwa.com.au/responsible-lighting-to-reduce-light-pollution/)
  recommends for night-sky reasons as well.
- **Output: the lowest level that does the job.** Most residential fittings sit
  between 2 W and 6 W. In doubt, buy dimmable or buy lower — nobody has ever
  complained that a garden was too subtle at night.
- **Beam angle: match the beam to the shape of the object.** Roughly 12–17
  degrees for a tall palm trunk or column, around 24 degrees for a tree, 35–38
  degrees for a facade or a smaller tree, 60 degrees as the general-purpose angle
  for planting, and 110 degrees to wash a broad wall
  ([VOLT Lighting](https://www.voltlighting.com/learn/selecting-beam-spreads)).

## Glare and spill: draw the neighbours in

Glare is seeing the lamp instead of the lit object. Spill is your light landing
on someone else's property. Both are design faults, and both are cheaper to fix
on paper than on site.

Put the neighbours on the drawing — their windows, their alfresco, the footpath.
On a typical Perth block an uplight under a tree can be six metres from a bedroom
window on the other side of the fence, and the fence stops nothing above head
height. Then write four rules on the sheet as notes:

1. **Nothing aimed above the horizontal** unless the beam is stopped by a solid
   object — a wall, a canopy, a trunk.
2. **Shield everything.** Cowls, hoods, hex louvres, recessed lamps. A bare LED
   chip visible from anywhere people stand is a fault, not a style.
3. **Mount low and aim away** from boundaries, windows and the street.
4. **Switch by function.** Steps and thresholds on one circuit that can run all
   evening; features and task lighting on a second that shuts off at 22:00.
   Dusk-to-dawn everything is lazy, and it is what annoys neighbours.

## The cable plan, which is really a trenching plan

This is the half that must be finished before earthworks start.

- **Draw every run from the transformer, with a length.** Lengths drive cable
  size — the voltage drop and transformer sizing calculations live in
  [garden lighting installation](/curriculum/build/garden-lighting/), and you need
  them before you buy cable.
- **Sleeve under every hard surface, plus one spare sleeve you have no use for
  yet.** Cheap now, a saw cut later. Coordinate with
  [base preparation](/curriculum/build/base-preparation/).
- **Keep lighting cable out of the retic trench.** Offset 300 mm or run it in
  conduit, or the shovel that replaces a solenoid in three years finds the cable.
- **Show a 500 mm service loop** at each fixture, so fittings can move once the
  planting grows in.
- **Do not take a burial depth off a blog.** AS/NZS 3000 sets minimum depths of
  cover; ask your electrical contractor for the figure that applies to your cable
  and conduit, and put that number on the drawing.

Then build the fixture schedule on the same sheet: reference, type, location,
wattage, colour temperature, beam angle, IP rating, mounting, circuit, and ELV or
mains. That table is what
[quantity take-offs](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/) prices and what the
[specification](/curriculum/documentation/specification-and-schedule-of-works/)
turns into an order.

## Renovating: what is already in the ground

- **Existing 240 V garden lights with no paperwork.** Spike floodlights and
  bollards installed by a previous owner may or may not have been done by a
  licensed contractor, and you cannot tell by looking. Show them on the plan as
  "inspect and certify or remove". Until an electrician has looked, treat them as
  an electrocution risk in a wet bed and a disclosure problem at sale.
- **An old halogen transformer.** Iron-core units sized for halogen often specify
  a minimum load and will not regulate properly with a handful of LED fittings.
  Converting to LED means a new transformer too.
- **Anything within 1.25 m of the pool.** Metal light fittings that close to the
  water must be bonded to the main earthing system by a licensed electrical
  contractor. Separately, AS 1926.1-2012 governs what may sit near a barrier,
  including anything climbable — confirm clearances with your local government's
  pool inspector before you place a bollard, planter or step light near the
  fence, and read
  [pools and pool fencing](/curriculum/approvals/pools-and-pool-fencing/).
- **Fixtures worth salvaging.** Solid brass and 316 stainless fittings from your
  [existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/)
  are worth keeping and re-lamping. Powder-coated aluminium that has been through
  a few Perth summers is not.
