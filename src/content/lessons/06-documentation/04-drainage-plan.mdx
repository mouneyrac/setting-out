The councils that have a stormwater network mostly will not let you into it. The answer in Perth is infiltration: you put the water into the ground on your own block, in a hole, at a rate the sand can take. That is what a soakwell is, and it is why every drainage plan in this city starts with an area measurement and a multiplication.

The rule you multiply by is set by your local government, and it is not the same everywhere. That single fact defeats most people who look this up online.

Two safety points before anything else. A soakwell excavation is a small, deep hole in cohesionless sand, and WorkSafe WA treats 1.5 metres as the depth at which a rescue plan is required. In 2018 a plumber drowned in a 2.2 to 3 metre trench at Mosman Park when a nearby water main burst; the companies involved were fined [$720,000](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/announcements/construction-and-plumbing-companies-fined-total-of-720000-over-mosman-park-shopping-centre-trench-collapse). Never enter an unsupported excavation in Perth sand, and never work one alone. Second: stormwater is not licensed plumbing work in WA, but sewer drainage is. If your plan touches the sewer, that is a licensed plumber's job, and if a Water Corporation sewer crosses your block you must [locate it before you design](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/developing-and-building/working-near-assets/building-on-a-residential-property), not before you dig.

## What a professional does at this stage

They size and place the drainage before they finalise the levels, not after.

The reason is a chain of dependencies. Soakwell depth is limited by the watertable. Soakwell top level sets the invert of the pipe entering it. That invert plus the fall sets the level of every grate and spoon drain feeding it. Those levels set the paving levels, which set the threshold heights at the doors. Design the paving first and you end up either pumping, or cutting a 300 mm step nobody wanted, or quietly running the fall the wrong way.

They also fix soakwell positions before the paving layout. A soakwell buried under finished limestone paving cannot be maintained, and every council requires maintenance access. Retrofitting one later costs the paving as well as the well.

On a renovation there is a third job: prove what is already there. Probe for existing wells, lift the lids, and look. Perth soakwells silt up, fill with roots, or turn out to be a 1970s brick shaft half full of sand. Do not count capacity you have not seen working.

## Step 1: measure the impervious area

Impervious area is everything that sheds water instead of absorbing it: roof, driveway, paving, concrete, the alfresco slab, the pool surround. Measure each off your [layout plan](/curriculum/documentation/scaled-layout-plan/) and total them.

The trap on a renovation is invisible. Replacing 90 square metres of lawn with limestone paving adds 90 square metres of impervious area to a block whose drainage was sized without it. Nothing about the house changed, so nobody checks, and the alfresco floods in the first real May storm. Measure the **new** impervious area separately, and expect a council to assess the whole site at development approval.

## Step 2: size the soakwells to your council's rule

The common Perth rule is impervious area multiplied by a depth of rainfall. What varies is the depth.

| Local government | Rule | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [City of Stirling](https://www.stirling.wa.gov.au/awcontent/Web/Documents/Developing%20Property/Building%20documents/City-of-Stirling-On-Site-Drainage-Criteria-June-2024-1.pdf) | Impervious area (m²) × 0.015 m | 1 in 1 ARI, 60 minute event = 15 mm across the site |
| [Town of Claremont](https://www.claremont.wa.gov.au/media/yqebmx4o/info-sheet-requirements-for-on-site-stormwater-drainage.pdf), [Town of East Fremantle](https://www.eastfremantle.wa.gov.au/Profiles/eastfremantle/Assets/ClientData/Document-Centre/planning-approval/ToEF_-_Specification-for-stormwater-requirement-for-on-site-drainage_v3_0.pdf) | Impervious area (m²) × 0.0125 m | 12.5 mm |
| [City of Joondalup](<https://www.joondalup.wa.gov.au/plan-and-build/residential-building-and-renovation-guides/residential-soakwells-(stormwater-runoff)>) | Published capacity table, no formula | Roughly 14 m² per 180 litres |
| [City of Rockingham](https://rockingham.wa.gov.au/planning-and-building/building-and-renovating/soakwells) | No published formula; setback rule only | Contain all stormwater on the lot |

Worked example. You are adding a 60 square metre alfresco roof and 120 square metres of paving — 180 square metres of new impervious area.

- Stirling rule: 180 × 0.015 = **2.70 m³**. Two 1200 mm diameter × 1200 mm deep wells hold 1.36 m³ each, so two of them (2.72 m³) does it.
- Claremont rule: 180 × 0.0125 = **2.25 m³**. A 1200 × 1200 plus a 1200 × 900 (1.36 + 1.02 = 2.38 m³) does it.

Three rules come with the arithmetic. Stirling sets a minimum of 900 mm diameter × 600 mm deep for roof water. Stirling also requires roof runoff to be stored separately from paving runoff, which means two sets of wells, not one. And a soakwell must sit above the maximum groundwater level — check the depth to watertable at your address on the [Perth Groundwater Map](https://maps.water.wa.gov.au/Groundwater/) before you decide how deep to go. Water still ponding after [96 hours](https://www.wa.gov.au/system/files/2022-07/Water-sensitive-urban-design-Soakwells.pdf) between November and May is a mosquito breeding problem as well as a failed soakwell.

**Confirm the current figure with your own council before you calculate.** Ring the building services counter, ask for their on-site stormwater drainage requirements sheet, and use the number on it.

## Step 3: place them legally

Setbacks vary as much as the sizing does, and they are the reason soakwells end up in front lawns.

| Local government | Setback from buildings and boundaries |
| --- | --- |
| City of Stirling | At least 1 m edge to edge, or the manufacturer's specification, whichever is greater |
| Town of Claremont, Town of East Fremantle | 1 m or the soakwell's own depth, whichever is greater, from the outside edge to a footing or boundary |
| City of Joondalup | Minimum the width of the soakwell |
| City of Rockingham | Not less than the depth of the soakwell, measured from ground level |

Design to the strictest rule you might be held to: a 1200 mm deep well gets 1.2 metres clear of every footing and boundary. Then check the sewer, and check what is above — you cannot put a soakwell under a tree you are keeping.

One legal point catches renovators. Under the Building Act 2011, work that adversely affects the **site drainage of adjoining land** requires that neighbour's consent, or a court order. Raising a level, redirecting a fall, or dumping your overflow at the fence line all qualify. Discharging onto a neighbour or the street is not a grey area — you must confine runoff within your boundaries.

## Step 4: pipework and falls

Every pipe on your plan needs a stated fall, written as a ratio, with invert levels at both ends taken from your [levels and grading plan](/curriculum/documentation/levels-and-grading-plan/).

Surface falls come first, because most of a Perth garden drains across the ground rather than through a pipe. The [ABCB Housing Provisions](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions/3-site-preparation/part-33-drainage) require impermeable surfaces to fall 25 mm over the first metre from the building in a low rainfall intensity area, and 50 mm over the first metre in any other case. Design to 50 mm and you satisfy both. The same part sets finished ground 100 mm below the slab edge in sandy, well-drained areas, or 50 mm below where an impermeable surface slopes away.

For connecting pipework, AS/NZS 3500.3:2025 sets the minimum grade for each pipe size — reference it on your drawing rather than reproducing it. In practice 1:100 (10 mm per metre) on a 90 or 100 mm line is easy to build, easy to check with a laser, and comfortably above the minimum. Cover depths for 90 mm Class 6 uPVC are 100 mm under soil, 50 mm under paving or concrete, and 100 mm under paving carrying light vehicle traffic.

Also mark every downpipe and where it goes, a grated inlet before each below-ground system (Stirling requires this), trafficable lids anywhere a well sits under paving or a driveway, and the overflow path for the storm that beats your design.

## Step 5: subsoil drains and their outlets

Surface drainage moves water off a surface. Subsoil drainage moves water out of the ground, and on the Swan Coastal Plain you need it wherever something stops water going down — a clay lens, coffee rock under Bassendean sand, a compacted fill pad, or an old building slab. Revisit your [soil profile](/curriculum/site-analysis/perth-soils/) and [water on site](/curriculum/site-analysis/water-on-site/) notes.

Mark a subsoil drain behind every retaining wall, at the base of every cut, and around any sunken courtyard or firepit. Slotted 100 mm pipe in a geotextile sock, in clean aggregate, wrapped in geotextile, graded at not less than 1:300 uniform fall.

Then the part people forget: **draw the outlet.** A subsoil drain with nowhere to discharge is a trench that fills up and stays full. It should discharge through a silt pit or sump, with the outlet at least 50 mm below the invert of the inlet, into a separate soakwell or a lower point on your own land. Never into the base of a stormwater soakwell that is already full during the storm.

The soakwell sizes, pipe runs and excavation volumes become line items in your [quantity take-offs](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/). The invert levels lock the finished levels for paving. And the whole drawing is what gets built, and checked, in [drainage and soakwells](/curriculum/build/drainage-and-soakwells/).
