Irrigation is the only service in a garden that has to be drawn before the
hardscape is built and cannot be corrected afterwards without breaking
something. A planting mistake is a plant. A retic mistake is a mainline under
250 square metres of new liquid limestone.

This lesson produces the drawing. It does not teach the hydraulics — that is the
[irrigation mini-course in Phase 8](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydraulics/),
and you should read at least
[hydraulics](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydraulics/) and
[hydrozoning and stations](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydrozoning-and-stations/)
before you commit anything here.

One legal point first. The connection at the head of your mainline is water
supply plumbing, and in WA installing or altering water supply plumbing is
**major plumbing work** under the Plumbers Licensing and Plumbing Standards
Regulations 2000 — a new or replacement testable backflow prevention device
always counts. That is a licensed plumber, not you. Draw the connection, specify
the device, and put a plumber in the schedule of works. Check licences on the
[Building and Energy licence search](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-and-energy-licence-and-registration-search),
which also covers the electrician you need for a 240 V controller supply or a
pump circuit.

## What a professional does at this stage

They design backwards from the watering window, and they draw the mainline
before they draw a single sprinkler.

Most people start with where the heads go. A professional starts with how many
minutes they are legally allowed, because that number caps the station count,
which caps how finely they can hydrozone — which sometimes sends them back to
change the design.

The mainline and the sleeves under paving go in during earthworks, before the
base is compacted. If the plan does not exist by then, someone trenches through
finished work later. And because the irrigator, the plumber and
you-in-five-years all have to read it, every valve gets a number and every
mainline run gets an offset dimension from a permanent edge. That drawing
becomes the [as-built](/curriculum/handover/as-built-drawings/).

## Start with the run time budget

Perth is on **two rostered watering days a week**, once per day, **before 9am or
after 6pm**, and bore users get the same roster and hours. Look up your own days
on [Check your watering days](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/check-your-watering-days);
breaching it is a $100 on-the-spot fine, and the
[roster and winter ban lesson](/curriculum/approvals/bores-roster-and-the-winter-ban/)
covers the rules in full. Treat the pre-9am window as your budget, take run
times from the Water Corporation's published application rates, and add them up:

| Station | Hydrozone | Emitter | Run time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Back lawn | Gear drive rotary | 35 min |
| 2 | Front lawn | Gear drive rotary | 35 min |
| 3 | Feature beds, west sun | Dripline | 35 min |
| 4 | Feature beds, south shade | Dripline | 30 min |
| 5 | Native / low-water bed | Dripline | 30 min |
| 6 | Pots and entry | Micro spray | 15 min |
| | | **Total** | **3 h 00 min** |

Start at 5:30am and it finishes at 8:30am, inside the window. A seventh station
pushes the 9am boundary. Cycle-and-soak on deep Bassendean sand needs you to be
cleverer, not longer: split the lawn stations into two shorter passes and
**order the program so the other stations run during the soak interval**. Total
minutes barely change; the water gets time to move down instead of running off.

If it does not fit: increase available flow so stations can be bigger, shrink
the high-water hydrozone, merge two areas that genuinely share a water
requirement, or accept manual care for one small area. A second start time is
not an option — the rule is once per rostered day. If you are unsure whether
cycle-and-soak counts as one watering event, ring Water Corporation on 13 10 39
rather than guessing.

## Stations: one hydrozone, one emitter type, one aspect

Anything sharing a solenoid gets the same minutes, so anything with a different
water requirement needs a different solenoid.

| Never share a station | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Lawn and garden bed | Different rates, different depths, different schedules |
| Spray and rotary heads | 35–45 mm/h against 10–20 mm/h on the same valve |
| Full sun and deep shade | Same plant, twice the demand |
| Dripline and sprinklers | Completely different application logic |
| Slope top and slope bottom | Bottom stays wet, top stays dry |
| Established natives and new planting | The new planting needs establishment water the natives will rot in |

That last row matters on a renovation. If you are keeping mature plants and
adding new beds, the new beds get their own stations — you will run them hard
for the first summer and then wind them back, which is impossible on a shared
valve.

Carry the zone boundaries straight off the
[hydrozone plan](/curriculum/concept-design/hydrozoning/). Do not redraw them by
eye here.

## The mainline: route it where nobody will ever dig again

The mainline is pressurised whenever the system is on. A lateral leaks only
while its station runs; a mainline leak runs 24 hours a day into sand, and you
find out via the bill.

1. **Lodge a [BYDA request](https://www.byda.com.au/duty-of-care/) first.** Free,
   and it shows where the water service, gas, power and comms run. Hand-dig to
   expose anything near the route. On a renovation, assume BYDA misses the
   previous owner's unmarked poly and the dead power run to the shed —
   cross-reference your own
   [services survey](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/).
2. **Run it straight, parallel to a permanent edge, offset dimensioned.**
   "1.2 m off the southern boundary fence" is findable in ten years. A curve
   through a lawn is not.
3. **Never under future paving, concrete, decking or a slab.** Cross at right
   angles, inside a sleeve.
4. **Sleeve every hard-surface crossing and oversize it.** 100 mm conduit,
   capped, both ends staked and on the plan, plus a separate sleeve for the
   [lighting cable](/curriculum/documentation/lighting-plan/). A few dollars a
   metre now; several hundred to retrofit through a finished driveway.
5. **Keep out of the drainage.** Overlay the
   [drainage plan](/curriculum/documentation/drainage-plan/) and stay clear of
   soakwell excavations and stormwater trenches.
6. **Keep out of the root plate of trees you are keeping**, and out of anywhere
   you will cultivate deeply.

Perth trade practice is a deeper trench for the mainline than for laterals.
Confirm the cover depth your supplier and installer specify, and put that number
on the drawing.

## Valve boxes you can actually open

- **Group valves into manifolds** of two to four in one rectangular box rather
  than scattering single valves. Fewer places to look, one wiring run.
- **Boxes go in mulched garden bed** — never in lawn, never inside a paved area.
  A box in turf gets mown over, sinks and disappears.
- **Lid flush with finished level**, taken off the
  [levels and grading plan](/curriculum/documentation/levels-and-grading-plan/),
  not off today's ground, and with nothing large planted in front of it.
- **Number every valve on the drawing and on the lid.** Station 3 on the plan is
  the lid marked 3.
- **Show the master valve and controller position**, and note the wiring: one
  core per station, plus a common, plus at least two spares. Spare cores are the
  cheapest insurance in the system.

## The establishment program is part of the drawing

New planting cannot survive on two days a week in its first month, so Water
Corporation grants a **watering exemption** for a new lawn or garden — up to 42
days from installation between 1 October and 31 March, and up to 35 days between
1 April and 30 September. Breaching the conditions risks a **$500 fine**, and
the property owner carries that risk, not the installer. Apply on the day you
plant; the clock runs from installation, not application.

Put two programs on the drawing — an establishment schedule and the ongoing
schedule, with the changeover date — then add the Water Corporation's
[seasonal adjustment percentages](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/sprinklers-and-irrigation/why-you-need-to-adjust-your-irrigation)
as a table on the same sheet: 100% January and February, 80% March, 60% April,
manual only in May and September, off June to August for the Switch-off, 60%
October, 80% November, 90% December. One set of run times, one dial —
[scheduling and uniformity](/curriculum/build/irrigation-scheduling-and-uniformity/)
tunes those numbers against catch-cup data once the system is in. Note the
Switch-off on the drawing too: 1 June to 31 August, bores included, and if your
build lands in that window you are hand-watering unless your supply is a
rainwater tank.

## Safety on the day this gets built

Trenching in Perth sand is more dangerous than it looks: dry sand walls collapse
without warning, so keep trenches shallow and battered, and never put your head
below ground level in an unsupported trench. Hand-dig inside the exclusion zones
on your BYDA plans, do not work alone in a trench in summer heat, and leave the
connection and the backflow device to the licensed plumber.
