Every hour you work on your own block is an hour you do not pay for. That is the
whole appeal, and on a renovation it is stronger than usual, because you already
own the shovel and the weekend, and half the demolition is just carrying things
to a skip.

The trap is that the saving is not evenly spread. On some tasks you keep most of
the labour cost and nobody could tell your work from a contractor's. On others
you save nothing at all, because the work gets done twice — and the second time
you also pay for demolishing the first attempt and everything built on top of it.

This lesson is a way to tell those apart before you commit, using five scores in
a fixed order. The order matters: the first score is a gate, not a weighting.

## What a professional does at this stage

A landscape contractor does not self-perform everything either. Every
established Perth landscaping business subcontracts roughly the same list —
electrical, plumbing and gas, concrete pours, licensed asbestos removal, deep or
tight excavation, structural certification, often the retic — and keeps in-house
the work it does every week.

That is not a skills confession, it is a risk-adjusted cost decision made once
and applied to every job. The contractor knows what a rectification costs
because they have paid for one. You are making the same call with less data, so
make it more explicitly: take your
[quantity take-offs](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/) and score every
task line on five things.

## Score 1: does the law even let you? (a gate, not a score)

If the answer here is no, nothing else matters. This is where most DIY plans
quietly break in WA.

| Work | Who may do it | The number that matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Electrical** — outdoor GPOs, garden lighting circuits, pump wiring | Licensed electrician only | No homeowner exemption exists in WA at any skill level. Contractor licences start EC, electricians EW. Safety certificate within 28 days |
| **Plumbing** — any connection to the potable supply, backflow devices, drainage | Licensed plumber only | Five homeowner tasks are permitted, and none of them are outside. Certificate of compliance within 5 working days |
| **Gas** — outdoor kitchen, fire pit, heater | Licensed gas fitter only | Check the licence on the [Building and Energy register](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-and-energy-licence-and-registration-search) |
| **Asbestos removal** | You, under 10 m² non-friable | Over 10 m² non-friable needs a Class A or B licensee; any friable needs Class A |
| **Class 10a structures** — patio, pergola, carport, shed | You, from 1 July 2026, under $50,000 | Previously $20,000. A building permit is still required |
| **Other building work over $20,000 needing a permit** | Registered building contractor, or you with owner-builder approval | Owner-builder approval is once every six years |

Two traps. The 1 July 2026 change is real and useful — a homeowner can now build
their own patio under $50,000 without owner-builder approval — but it changed
*who may build*, not *whether you need a permit*. Read
[patios, pergolas, decks and sheds](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/)
before you assume anything. And the electrical rule has no soft edge: a lighting
circuit, an outdoor power point and the supply to a bore pump are all licensed
work. Budget the sparky at design stage — see
[garden lighting](/curriculum/build/garden-lighting/).

## Score 2: is the failure buried?

This is the score that decides most jobs, and the one amateurs skip. Ask: **when
this is wrong, when do I find out, and what has to come off to fix it?** A
mistake in mulch costs you a morning. A mistake in a compacted base costs you the
paving, the edge restraint and the labour sitting on top of it.

The general rule: *the earlier a task sits in the build sequence, the less you
should be learning on it.*

| Task | You find out | What comes off to fix it | Sensible default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drainage and soakwells | First serious winter, 1–2 years | Everything above them | Pay |
| Base preparation and compaction | 1–3 years, as dips and rocking | The whole surface | Pay, or pay for the first area and watch |
| Retaining over the exempt height | Years — or suddenly | The wall, the fill and the neighbour's goodwill | Pay, with engineering |
| Concrete and liquid limestone | Within hours, permanently | Jackhammer and cart away | Pay |
| Mainline, valves and wiring under paving | 2–5 years | The paving | Pay for the buried run |
| Setting out, levels and falls | At the first rain | Whatever you built to the wrong level | Pay for a check, DIY the rest |
| Paving laid on a correct base | Immediately | The pavers, which you keep | DIY is realistic |
| Decking above ground | Immediately | Boards, which unscrew | DIY is realistic |
| Soil preparation | One growing season | Nothing structural | DIY |
| Planting, mulching, edging, turf | Immediately | The plant | DIY |
| Drippers, risers and surface retic | Immediately | Nothing | DIY |

Read that table as one sentence: **subcontract the buried layer, do the visible
layer yourself.** Drainage is the sharpest case on the Swan Coastal Plain —
soakwells are cheap to install and ruinously expensive to retrofit, because
retrofitting means lifting finished surfaces. Get the sizing right on paper
first ([drainage plan](/curriculum/documentation/drainage-plan/)), then decide
who digs.

## Score 3: tools, and the honest arithmetic of hire

The question is not "can I hire it", it is "how many hire days will this take
me". A crew does in two days what you do across five weekends: five hire days on
the same plate compactor, five pickups, five returns. Get a current daily and
weekly rate from your hire branch before it becomes an assumption in your cost
plan.

Two things to know before you book anything. **Compaction gear is hireable; the
judgement is not** — crushed limestone compacted in one 200 mm lift instead of
two 100 mm lifts looks identical and fails in two years
([base preparation](/curriculum/build/base-preparation/)). And **machinery is
decided by access, not enthusiasm** — width, overhead clearance, and what is
buried. Work through [machinery](/curriculum/build/machinery/) and
[locating services](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/) first, and
lodge a free [Before You Dig](https://www.byda.com.au/) enquiry regardless of who
ends up digging.

## Score 4: what can actually hurt you

WorkSafe's duties are written for employers, main contractors and self-employed
persons. Alone on your own block on a Saturday, nobody is coming to stop you.
That is precisely the problem: the physics does not check your employment
status.

- **Trenches.** WorkSafe's excavation code says workers must not be required to
  work in an excavation 1.5 metres or more deep without shoring, and that a
  second person must be in the immediate vicinity at that depth. Its own figure
  for why: the weight of a wedge of sand over a one metre length of a two metre
  deep trench is about three tonnes — more than enough to crush a chest. Perth
  sand has no cohesion. It does not lean, it goes.
- **Silica.** Anything with at least 1 per cent crystalline silica counts, which
  is every concrete paver, limestone block, brick and tile on your site. Dry
  cutting without water suppression or on-tool extraction is the exposure that
  causes silicosis. Cut wet, outdoors, upwind, in a fitted P2 respirator, and
  keep everyone else away.
- **Asbestos.** Pre-1990 Perth blocks: super six fencing, fibro sheds, old
  eaves. Do not cut, drill or water-blast it. Over 10 m² of non-friable material
  is licensed removal work.
- **Heat.** Perth summer earthworks are genuinely dangerous when you are unfit
  and alone. Schedule the heavy days for the cooler months — conveniently, when
  planting wants to happen anyway.

In a hole, above head height, or holding a spinning blade against masonry is not
automatically a subcontract, but it is automatically a "not alone, not
improvised" job. See
[site establishment and safety](/curriculum/build/site-establishment-and-safety/).

## Score 5: the value of your own time

Cost your hours at something, even if you never pay it. Sixty hours of weekends
is most of a summer. If the DIY saving on a task is small and the hours are
large, that is a subcontract with extra steps. Do this honestly in
[labour, plant hire and the costs beginners forget](/curriculum/costing/labour-hire-and-hidden-costs/) —
a DIY line item with a blank labour column is not a comparison, it is a wish.

## The hybrids that actually work

You are not choosing between two columns.

- **Pay for the first one and watch.** Pay a retic installer to build zone one
  properly while you hold the trench line, then build zones two and three to the
  same pattern.
- **Buy a check, not a build.** Two hours of a professional's time verifying your
  set-out, levels and falls is the highest-value money in this lesson.
- **Split the layer, not the area.** Contractor does excavation, drainage and
  base; you do laying, planting and finishing. Cleaner than "their half and my
  half".
- **Supply only versus supply and fix.** Buying materials yourself saves the
  contractor's margin on materials but hands you every short delivery. Only where
  you trust your take-off.

One warning: **a trade will not warrant work sitting on your preparation.** If
you compact the base and they lay the paving, their guarantee covers laying only.
Get that boundary into the written scope, not agreed in the driveway —
[contracts and variations](/curriculum/costing/contracts-and-variations/) covers
how.

## Buying the subcontracted half properly

Check the licence number on the register, not the name on the ute. Get three
written quotes, and know that a quote binds and an estimate does not. Do not hand
over more than 10 per cent as a deposit on general trade work, or 6.5 per cent on
a building contract valued between $7,500 and $500,000.

For irrigation, the [Waterwise Programs directory](https://www.waterwiseprograms.com.au/find-a-water-specialist)
is the filter worth using. For landscaping itself there is no WA licence category
at all, so references, an insurance certificate and a written scope are your only
protection.

## What this changes downstream

Every task now carries a DIY or subcontract flag, and that flag changes two
numbers: the cost line and the duration. DIY is usually cheaper, always slower,
and can only happen on weekends — which reshapes
[staging the project](/curriculum/costing/staging-the-project/) and the order in
which trades can be booked.

Phase 8 starts building. The first lesson sets up the site so the work you have
just decided to do yourself does not injure you or damage the house.
