Your [take-off](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/) can be accurate and your
[material prices](/curriculum/costing/perth-material-prices/) can be current, and
the job will still finish over budget. That is not bad luck. The overrun lives in
a predictable set of lines that never appear on a drawing, so nobody measures
them: getting machinery in, getting waste out, getting materials delivered twice,
buying the tool you did not own, and the hundreds of hours of your own labour you
told yourself were free.

On a renovation these lines are bigger than on a new block, because you are
paying to remove somebody else's garden before you build yours, and because the
access was designed for a wheelbarrow in 1998.

## What a professional does at this stage

A landscaper prices the boring half of the job first. Before they price a single
paver they have settled how the machine gets in, where the spoil goes, what the
disposal weighs, and how many days a crew is on site — because those four answers
change everything else, and finding out late turns a profitable job into a
break-even one.

They also carry a **preliminaries** line: the cost of running the site rather
than building anything. Set-up, protection of what stays, fuel, consumables,
travel, waste, cleaning up. That is not a rounding error, and a quote without it
is a quote that will be varied.

The third habit is the one worth copying: they know their crew's daily output
because they have measured it. Not "a day should do it" — actual square metres of
paving laid per person per day, in Perth heat. You do not have that data, which
is exactly why your own hours need a deliberate pessimistic allowance.

## Access: measure it before you book anything

The most expensive beginner mistake in this phase is booking a machine that
cannot reach the work. The float arrives, the machine will not fit past the meter
box, and you pay delivery and pick-up for a machine that never came off the
trailer — plus the lost day, plus a rebooking.

So before any plant appears in your cost plan, walk the access route with a tape
and write down, in millimetres, the narrowest gap, the lowest overhead, the
tightest turn and any step. Then get the transport width of the specific model
from the depot in writing. [Machinery](/curriculum/build/machinery/) covers the
machines; here it is a costing input, with three consequences:

1. **If the machine does not fit, the work becomes hand labour.** Barrowing sand
   through a 900 mm gate is many times slower than a mini loader. That is a
   labour line, not a hire line.
2. **A machine one size too small is not cheaper.** Same day rate, half the
   output — and Perth limestone at 400 mm will stop a 1.7 tonne machine dead.
3. **The hire "day" is 24 hours, not eight.** Delivery and pick-up are two
   separate float movements and are frequently the largest part of a one-day
   hire ([Kennards Hire rates FAQs](https://www.kennards.com.au/help-centre/rates-and-payment-faqs)).

Booking the machine twice — once for demolition, again for base prep — pays that
float twice. Sequencing it into one visit is what
[staging the project](/curriculum/costing/staging-the-project/) is for.

## Waste: the number nobody guesses correctly

Disposal is priced by what it weighs and what it is mixed with. The WA waste levy
sits underneath every gate fee in the metropolitan area — the
[published rate schedule](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/environment/environment-information-services/waste-levy-rate-schedule)
lists $90 per tonne, or $136 per cubic metre, from 1 July 2026, though only the
2025-26 rates are marked there as prescribed by regulation — and the levy is only
charged on material that goes to landfill. That is the whole
reason a sorted load is cheaper: sorted material is recovered, not buried.

Four metropolitan facilities, 2026/27 published rates, showing how differently
the same load can price:

| Facility | Green waste | Mixed / general | Clean rubble and concrete |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Tamala Park](https://www.mrc.wa.gov.au/tamala-park/disposal/fees-charges.aspx) (Mindarie) | $291.50/t | $291.50/t | not listed — ring first |
| [Red Hill](https://www.emrc.org.au/our-services-and-products/our-services/fees-and-charges.aspx) (EMRC) | $98/t commercial, $65/t member resident, $30/t pre-shredded | $236/t | not listed separately |
| [Ranford Road](https://www.canning.wa.gov.au/residents/waste-and-recycling/ranford-road-resource-recovery-and-waste-transfer-station/) (Canning resident) | $28 under 300 kg, then $63/t | $61 under 200 kg, $122 for 201–400 kg, then $277/t | $30 under 200 kg, $53 for 201–400 kg, then $102/t |
| [West Metro](https://www.wmrc.wa.gov.au/recycling-disposal/west-metro-recycling-centre/fees-charges/) (Shenton Park) | $45/m³ member, $60/m³ other | $70/m³ member, $85/m³ other | C&D $70/m³ member, $80/m³ other |

Read four things off that table.

**Sorting is worth more than any material discount you will negotiate.** At
Ranford Road, keeping rubble clean takes a resident load over 400 kg from $277 to
$102 per tonne. Sort at the source — separate piles on tarps from day one —
because nobody sorts a mixed heap afterwards.

**Not every site rewards sorting.** Tamala Park charges green waste at the same
rate as general waste, so in the northern suburbs your green waste strategy is
your council's bulk garden organics verge collection, chipping on site, or a
mulching contractor — not a trailer to Mindarie. Check your own council: the
[City of Canning](https://www.canning.wa.gov.au/residents/waste-and-recycling/),
for one, gives residents two garden organics verge collections a year.

**Residency changes the price.** Resident rates need proof of address, and a
contractor taking your waste away pays the commercial column and passes it on
with margin. "And we'll take the rubbish" is never free.

**Weight, not appearance, fills a bin.** A cubic metre of broken concrete or
limestone is close to two tonnes, so most skip bins hit their weight cap long
before they look full and the excess is charged. Get the weight allowance and the
excess rate in writing, and order a separate small bin for rubble rather than one
big mixed one.

Asbestos is a different market entirely —
[$450 per tonne at Tamala Park](https://www.mrc.wa.gov.au/tamala-park/disposal/fees-charges.aspx),
[$330 per tonne wrapped at Red Hill](https://www.emrc.org.au/our-services-and-products/our-services/fees-and-charges.aspx)
on the commercial rate, accepted only at nominated sites and only pre-booked.
If your audit flagged super six fencing or a fibro shed, that is its own line
item. See [demolition and clearing](/curriculum/build/demolition-and-clearing/).

## Deliveries, and why the second one hurts

A delivery fee is per truck movement, not per cubic metre. Order 10 per cent
short and the top-up load costs nearly what the first one did, so round every
bulk order up and confirm minimum load quantities before you split an order
across suppliers.

Dense material is limited by weight and light material by volume, so one Perth
yard's small truck carries
[2.5 m³ of sand but 4 m³ of mulch](https://www.bibralakesoils.com.au/delivery/)
and its large truck 7 m³ or 10 m³. The truck also has to be able to tip. A verge
drop is fine; a drop behind a 900 mm gate is not, and a driver who cannot tip
leaves you paying for a hand-unload or a refused delivery. Ask where the driver
will actually tip, because loaded tippers and tracked machines damage kerbs and
footpaths and Perth councils will bill you for the crossover repair.

## Tools you do not own yet

Every hardscape job has a tool line, and it is always larger than expected
because the tool needs consumables and the work needs controls.

| Item | Typical answer | The part people forget |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plate compactor | Hire by the day | Multiple visits — base, then paving. Two hires, or one long one |
| Brick or demolition saw | Hire | Diamond blades are consumable and charged per blade worn |
| Silica dust control | Buy | Water feed, fitted P2 or better to AS/NZS 1715, H-class vacuum |
| Laser level | Hire or buy | Useless without a staff and a tripod; check what the hire includes |
| Wheelbarrow, shovels, rake, screed rails | Buy | The cheap ones fail mid-job, on a Sunday |
| PPE | Buy | Gloves, eye protection, hearing protection, sun protection, boots |

Cutting pavers, limestone, brick or concrete releases respirable crystalline
silica. [WorkSafe WA](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/managing-crystalline-silica-dust-workplace)
requires wet cutting or on-tool extraction, respiratory protection selected under
AS/NZS 1715, and vacuum rather than broom clean-up. A hardware-aisle dust mask is
not compliance and is not protection. Budget the controls with the saw or do not
budget the saw.

## Labour: theirs and yours

The legal floor for landscaping labour in Australia from 1 July 2026 is
[$26.44 an hour for a level 1 gardener or landscaper, plus a 25 per cent casual
loading](https://awards.fairwork.gov.au/MA000101.html). That is the minimum wage,
not the cost. On top of an employed hour sit superannuation, workers
compensation, leave, tools, vehicle, fuel and every hour spent travelling,
loading and cleaning up. That is why a contractor's charge-out rate is several
times the award figure, and why it is not a rip-off.

It is also why paying a mate cash is a false economy. If they are hurt on your
block you have no insurer, no contract and no recourse, and you have paid market
rate for an uninsured amateur. If you engage anyone, ask for their ABN, a
certificate of currency for public liability, and evidence of workers
compensation cover for anyone they bring with them. No documents, no engagement.

**Costing your own hours is not optional.** Call your labour free and two things
go wrong: you choose the more laborious option every time because it looks cheaper
on paper, and you never find out that paying a contractor for two days would have
saved you six weekends. The method:

1. **Pick a rate and use it consistently.** Your own after-tax hourly rate, or
   what you would pay a labourer. Consistency matters more than the number.
2. **Estimate the hours, then multiply by 2.5.** First-timers work at roughly a
   third of trade speed and lose the rest to setting up, fetching and working out
   what to do next. That multiplier is calibration, not pessimism.
3. **Log actual hours as you go.** By the third task you have real data and your
   remaining estimates get sharp.
4. **Add a line for the calendar cost.** A project that eats every weekend for
   four months has a cost not measured in dollars, and it is the most common
   reason DIY landscapes end up half finished.

Run those hours into [DIY versus subcontract](/curriculum/costing/diy-vs-subcontract/)
and some jobs will flip. That is the point of doing it.

## The lines that get left off

Sweep your cost plan for these before you call it finished: bin or tip trips
(count them, do not lump them), machine float charges both ways, delivery fees per
drop, fuel and blades, sand for backfill, wetting agent, stakes and ties,
temporary barriers, protection for surfaces you are keeping, council crossover or
verge damage, water for compaction and establishment, plant replacements in the
first summer, and the final clean-up — which on a renovation is a full day, not an
hour.
