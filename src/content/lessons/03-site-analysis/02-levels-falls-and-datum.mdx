Most Perth blocks look flat. Almost none of them are. Two hundred to four
hundred millimetres of fall across a 20 metre backyard is completely ordinary
on the coastal plain, and it is invisible to the naked eye — right up until you
lay a patio and discover the water runs back at the house.

On a renovation you are also inheriting somebody else's levels. Fifteen years
of mulch top-ups have raised the beds, the lawn has been sand-topped every
spring and now sits proud of the path, and a previous owner paved the side
access at whatever height suited the bricks. None of that shows in a
photograph. This lesson is about measuring what is actually there, in numbers
you can do arithmetic on, from a reference point you can come back to.

## Why a professional does this before drawing anything

A levels survey is the first piece of hard data a designer gets, and everything
downstream is arithmetic on top of it.

**It is the only way to design drainage legally.** In Western Australia you
must keep your stormwater on your own lot — the City of Wanneroo states it as a
statutory obligation under common law precedents and the *Local Government Act
1995* to confine water runoff within your property boundaries. You cannot
demonstrate that without levels.

**Councils ask for levels in AHD.** The Town of East Fremantle's onsite
drainage specification requires the site plan to show existing ground levels or
contours in AHD and proposed levels of paved or concrete areas in AHD. If your
project needs a development application, someone has to produce those numbers.

**It tells you whether you can build what you want.** A sunken firepit, a level
lawn, a deck flush with the back door — each is a levels problem before it is a
design problem. Fall you cannot lose is the most common reason a concept gets
redrawn.

**It finds the retaining early.** The moment the survey shows you need to cut
or fill more than about half a metre you are into approvals territory. Get that
news in week one, not after the concept is signed off — see
[retaining walls approval](/curriculum/approvals/retaining-walls-approval/).

The professional habit here is not the instrument. It is setting a datum first
and closing every level run back to it.

## The four ways to shoot levels

| Method | Cost | Practical accuracy over 20 m | People | Best for | Where it fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Water level** (garden hose plus two clear tube offcuts) | Materials only | Very good — the physics is exact, your reading of the meniscus is not; expect a few millimetres | Two, or one with clamps | Transferring a level around corners and through existing planting you cannot see across | Air bubbles, and one leg sitting in full Perth sun while the other is in shade |
| **String line and line level** | Cheapest option here | Poor — the line sags and the vial is tiny; 10 to 20 mm out is normal | One | A garden bed edge or a single step riser | Anything you intend to put on a drainage plan |
| **Rotary laser and detector** | Hire by the weekend, or buy | Good — a few millimetres, repeatably | One | Large open areas, and any job you are doing alone | Cheap cross-line lasers are useless outdoors here — you need a rotary laser with a staff-mounted detector to work in Perth daylight |
| **Automatic level ("dumpy") and staff** | Hire by the weekend, or buy | Best of the four for a garden survey | Two, or one with a staff bipod | Everything, and it is the method that teaches you the arithmetic | Needs a second person, and a bumped tripod invalidates every reading since the last backsight |

For a single block, hire. Perth yards stock both instruments — buying only pays
if you are staging the build over several years, which on a renovation you
probably are.

## Set the datum first

A datum is one point on your site that you assign an arbitrary height to and
never move. Every other level is expressed relative to it.

**Pick something permanent.** The best datum on a renovation is a feature of
the house itself: the finished floor level at a nominated doorway, or the top
of a specific step. It will not shift and you can find it again in December. If
you need a datum out in the garden, drive a 1.2 m star picket at least 600 mm
in and concrete the collar — a peg in Perth sand moves.

**Assign it RL 100.000.** Not zero. Surveyors use 100 so every level on the
site is a positive number and you never confuse "minus 0.24" with "0.24 below".
A point 240 mm lower is RL 99.760; 150 mm higher is RL 100.150.

**Photograph it and describe it in words.** "Datum = top of threshold, rear
sliding door, assigned RL 100.000" is a sentence you can act on in two years. A
paint mark alone is not.

**When you need real AHD.** An assumed datum is correct practice for a garden
survey. You need actual Australian Height Datum values if a development
application asks for them, if you are tying into a council drain, or if a
shallow watertable makes soakwell invert levels critical. Landgate publishes
the AHD heights of WA's benchmark network free; if no mark is within reach,
that is what surveyors are for.

## Taking the readings

Work on a grid — 5 m spacing is enough for open lawn, tighter where the ground
is obviously moving. Then take these regardless of where the grid falls:

- Every external door threshold, and the finished floor level inside it
- All four boundary corners, plus any change of direction
- Top and bottom of every existing retaining wall or step
- The invert (the bottom of the hole) of every soakwell, grate, gully and
  downpipe outlet you can find
- Top of kerb at the crossover, and the verge
- The neighbours' ground level on the far side of each fence
- The base of every tree you intend to keep

The last two matter more than people expect. You cannot legally dump your water
next door, and you cannot bury a mature tree's root flare in fill and expect it
to live.

### Booking the readings

Use the height of collimation method. Set the instrument up, take a backsight
onto the staff held on your datum, and add it:

```
Datum RL                   100.000
Backsight on datum           1.450
Height of collimation      101.450

Foresight at rear fence      1.780
RL at rear fence            99.670
```

Every subsequent reading is *height of collimation minus staff reading*.

**Close the run.** When you have finished, put the staff back on the datum and
read it again without moving the instrument. If it does not match your opening
backsight within about 5 mm, something moved and the whole run is suspect —
redo it. Professionals close every level run, and it is the single habit that
separates a survey you can build from a page of plausible numbers.

If you must move the instrument mid-survey, take a foresight on a solid
temporary point before you move, a backsight on that same point after, and
recalculate the height of collimation.

## Converting to falls

Fall is the difference between two reduced levels. What varies is how you
express it.

- **Fall in millimetres**: 100.000 − 99.760 = 240 mm
- **Percentage**: fall ÷ run × 100 → 240 ÷ 18 000 × 100 = **1.33%**
- **Ratio**: run ÷ fall → 18 000 ÷ 240 = **1:75**

All three describe the same slope. Contractors quote ratios, engineers quote
percentages, and the NCC quotes millimetres over the first metre. The
[RedBack slope and fall calculator](https://www.redbacklasers.com.au/slope-fall-calculator/)
converts instantly and flags your figure against the relevant Australian
Standard.

Targets worth knowing before you design:

| Situation | Target | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished ground away from the house — impermeable surface or access path | Minimum 25 mm over the first 1 m | NCC 2022 Housing Provisions 3.3.3(a) |
| Finished ground away from the house — all other cases | Minimum 50 mm over the first 1 m | NCC 2022 Housing Provisions 3.3.3(a) |
| Slab floor above finished ground, sandy well-drained site | Minimum 100 mm (150 mm generally) | NCC 2022 Housing Provisions 3.3.3(b) |
| Paved surface drainage | 1:100 is the standard minimum; build to 1:80 | AS/NZS 3500.3, as flagged by the RedBack calculator |
| Accessible ramp | 1:14 maximum | AS 1428.1, as flagged by the RedBack calculator |

Build to better than the minimum. A 1:100 patio is 1:100 on the day it is laid;
after settlement, construction tolerance and a decade of sand movement, it
ponds.

### The trap nobody warns you about

Falls compound over distance. A modest 1:80 across an 8 m patio is 100 mm of
drop. Run that same gradient the full 18 m to the back fence and you have lost
225 mm — which becomes a step, buries the bottom of the fence, or dumps into
the neighbour's yard. Draw a section through the longest run on your block
before you commit to a gradient. That is the calculation the
[levels and grading plan](/curriculum/documentation/levels-and-grading-plan/)
formalises.

### The renovation-specific check

Measure the clearance from your finished floor level down to the existing
ground and paving hard against the house. On older Perth homes gardened for
decades, that gap has usually been eaten away by accumulated mulch, soil and
re-paving. If you are down to 50 mm against a permeable garden bed, that is a
problem to solve in the design, not something to build on top of.

## Then go outside in the rain

Levels tell you where water *should* go. A June downpour tells you where it
*does*, and Perth's rain arrives in short heavy bursts, so the chance comes and
goes in twenty minutes. Put it in the calendar. Photograph the ponding; note
where water crosses the boundary in either direction, where it sheets off
paving, where the downpipes actually discharge, and whether the soakwells are
keeping up or surcharging. Mark all of it on the same base plan as the spot
levels — [water on site](/curriculum/site-analysis/water-on-site/) takes it
further.

## What this feeds

Your levels go onto the base plan from
[measuring up](/curriculum/site-analysis/measuring-up/), and together they
become the
[constraints and opportunities plan](/curriculum/site-analysis/constraints-and-opportunities-plan/)
at the end of this phase. Later, this survey is what the
[drainage plan](/curriculum/documentation/drainage-plan/) is calculated from
and what you set out against in
[earthworks and setting out](/curriculum/build/earthworks-and-setting-out/).

which decides how much of it you can move, and at what cost.
