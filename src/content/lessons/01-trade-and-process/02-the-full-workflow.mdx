A good landscaper's real advantage is not that they lay a straighter paver than
you. It is that they do things in an order that never requires undoing. Every
expensive disaster in this trade is a sequence error wearing a different costume:
the conduit that was not laid before the paving, the retaining wall built before
anyone rang the council, the plants bought before the soil budget existed.

This course is eleven phases long. That is not filing. The order is the content.
Read it once now for the shape, and come back whenever you are unsure what you
should be doing next.

## The eleven phases

| # | Phase | What you finish holding | What it stops you doing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The trade and the process | A map, and a definition of "finished" | Wandering |
| 2 | First contact and the brief | A written brief with a real budget number | Designing something you cannot afford |
| 3 | Site analysis | A measured base plan and a constraints plan | Designing on a site you have not read |
| 4 | Rules, permits and approvals | A written list of every approval you need | Falling in love with something illegal |
| 5 | Concept design | A concept plan answering the brief | Building a collection of features |
| 6 | Documentation | Drawings you can build and price from | Guessing on site, at 20 times the cost |
| 7 | Costing, quoting and contract | A staged, costed plan you can afford | Running out of money at 70 per cent |
| 8 | Build | The garden | Digging it up twice |
| 9 | Handover and records | As-builts, manual, warranties | Forgetting where the mainline runs |
| 10 | Maintenance and establishment | A calendar and a working retic | Watching the first summer kill it |
| 11 | Value and review | An honest review against the brief | Repeating the same mistake |

Notice where the drawing happens. Phase 5 of eleven. Four phases of unglamorous
work come first, and they are the cheap ones.

## Why the order is what it is

### Site analysis before concept design

A concept plan is a set of promises about a piece of ground. If you have not
measured the levels, dug a hole, tracked the afternoon sun and found the services,
every promise is a guess.

The Perth version: you draw a sunken fire pit, then hit cemented limestone at
400 mm and a rock breaker hire. You draw screening trees on the northern boundary,
then find the sewer runs there. You draw a lawn where the site falls toward the
house. None of these are design problems. They are information problems, and
information is cheapest before the design. That is what the
[constraints and opportunities plan](/curriculum/site-analysis/constraints-and-opportunities-plan/)
exists to force.

### Approvals before design commitment

Approvals sit at Phase 4, between analysis and design, for one reason: a
constraint you learn about late is a redesign, and a constraint you learn about
after construction is a demolition.

WA's rules bite in a suburban back garden. Retaining walls trigger permits at low
heights. Anything near a pool fence has its own regime. And registration and
permits are two separate questions people constantly merge: from 1 July 2026,
Class 10a buildings under $50,000 — patios, pergolas, carports, sheds — no longer
need a registered building contractor, but the
[building permit requirement is unchanged](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-or-renovating-your-home).
Above all of it sits the planning layer, the
[R-Codes](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/document-collections/residential-design-codes-volume-1-including-medium-density),
currently the April 2026 edition, in force since 10 April 2026.

Spend an afternoon in Phase 4 finding out what you may build, then design inside
that envelope. Start with [R-Codes and your council](/curriculum/approvals/r-codes-and-your-council/)
and [retaining walls approval](/curriculum/approvals/retaining-walls-approval/).

### Documentation before costing

You cannot price a picture. Quantities come off dimensioned drawings, which is
why Phase 6 sits before Phase 7 and why
[quantity take-offs](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/) is the first costing
lesson rather than the last. A concept plan will get you within about 40 per cent,
which is another way of saying it will get you nowhere.

### Irrigation before soil and plants

Inside Phase 8 the order is: services, levels, drainage, structure, hard surfaces,
**then irrigation, then soil, then plants, then mulch**.

Irrigation goes in before soil and plants because trenching a mainline through
finished beds destroys the soil profile you just paid for, and because plants must
be watered from the day they go in. Just as importantly, anything crossing under a
future path or lawn — conduit, sleeve, lighting cable — must be laid *before* that
base is compacted. Cutting a trench through new liquid limestone for a lateral you
forgot is a scar you own forever.

Soil goes on top of a working system, and plants go in last because they are the
only thing on site that dies if it waits. Follow this through
[irrigation hydraulics](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydraulics/) and
[turning Perth sand into soil](/curriculum/build/turning-perth-sand-into-soil/).

The other Perth-specific timing constraint is the sprinkler roster. Sprinklers are
banned from 1 June to 31 August across Perth, Mandurah and parts of the South West
and Great Southern, bore water included, and a new lawn or garden only gets an
exemption of up to 42 days (1 October to 31 March) or 35 days (1 April to
30 September) from the day of planting. Plant your lawn in May with no exemption
and you are legally unable to water it. See
[bores, roster and the winter ban](/curriculum/approvals/bores-roster-and-the-winter-ban/).

## Before you start: the parts that are not optional

Three things on a landscaping site can kill or permanently injure you, and none of
them care that it is your own house.

- **Buried services.** Lodge a free [Before You Dig Australia](https://www.byda.com.au/before-you-dig/for-homeowners/)
  enquiry before any excavation, then hand-dig near anything marked. Underground
  power on a Perth verge does not give you a second chance.
- **Silica dust.** Cutting pavers, brick, concrete or limestone releases
  respirable crystalline silica. Cut wet or with on-tool extraction, always, and
  wear a fitted respirator. Silicosis is irreversible.
- **Electrical work.** All electrical work in WA must be done by a licence holder,
  and it is illegal for an unlicensed person to carry out electrical work
  including modifications to fixed wiring. That covers mains-voltage garden
  lighting, pond pumps and a power point in a shed. You may lay the conduit; a
  licensed electrical contractor connects it and issues the certificate.

Trench collapse, working alone in 40-degree heat, tree work at height and asbestos
in pre-1990 sheds and fencing get their own treatment in
[site establishment and safety](/curriculum/build/site-establishment-and-safety/).

## What a professional does at this stage

A professional sells the sequence, not the shovel. Each phase produces an output
the next phase consumes, and they will not start a phase whose input is missing —
which is why a decent designer declines to draw until they have levels, and a
decent builder declines to start until the approvals are in hand.

The reason is cost of change. A decision changed on paper in Phase 5 costs an
afternoon. Changed in Phase 6 it costs a redraw and a re-price. In Phase 8 it
costs demolition, disposal, rework and a delay to every trade behind it. Roughly
an order of magnitude per phase — which is the whole argument for front-loading
the boring work.

They also write the output of each phase down. You are running one job, which
feels like an argument for keeping it in your head. It is not: renovations run for
months, and October-you will not remember what March-you decided.

## The three phases where projects most often go wrong

**Phase 4, approvals.** Skipped or assumed. A retaining wall or patio built
without the permit it needed is not a paperwork problem — councils can require it
to come out, and it is discoverable at sale. Cheapest phase to do properly, most
expensive to skip.

**Phase 7, costing and staging.** The classic Perth half-finished back garden:
paving and a pergola done, then nothing for three years because the money ran out
before soil, retic and plants. Costing is not optimism with a calculator, and
[staging](/curriculum/costing/staging-the-project/) means each stage has to be a
usable, finished thing on its own.

**Phase 8, build.** Sequence errors, and they are unforgiving. No sleeves under
the paving. Soil in before the retic. Turf laid in May with no exemption. Each one
is cheap to avoid and expensive to fix.

## Renovating changes the emphasis, not the order

An existing garden adds one heavy job to Phase 3 — the
[existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/) —
and one real budget line to Phase 8, because demolition and disposal cost money
and tip fees are charged by the tonne. It also gives you an advantage: you already
know how the block behaves in February, where water pools, and which corner nobody
uses. That is site analysis you did by living there.
