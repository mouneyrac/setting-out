Four different jobs share the word "landscaping" in Perth. They charge
differently, think differently and carry completely different legal exposure.
On your own renovation you will play all four in sequence, plus a fifth role
nobody warns you about — client.

The risk is not spread evenly. Most of what you are about to do is
unregulated: you may design, dig, pave, plant and mulch your own block with no
qualification whatsoever. A small part of it will get you prosecuted,
uninsured, or killed. Knowing exactly where that line sits is this lesson.

## The four roles

| Role | Paid to produce | Typical engagement | Registered in WA? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Landscape architect** | A documented design set for a complex or public site — site planning, levels, structures, specification, and often contract administration | Fee for a defined design stage, or a percentage of construction cost | No board registers landscape architects. Note that the plain title "architect" is protected under the Architects Act 2004 and controlled by the [Architects Board of WA](https://architectsboard.org.au/faqs/) |
| **Landscape designer** | A resolved garden design — concept plan, planting plan, materials and finishes schedule | Fixed fee per design stage, or hourly | No. No qualification is required to use the title |
| **Landscaper / landscape contractor** | The built garden — the physical work, and the coordination of the trades in it | Fixed-price contract, or day rate plus materials | Only once building work needing a permit passes the value thresholds below |
| **Horticulturist** | Plant knowledge — species selection for your soil and aspect, plant health diagnosis, the maintenance regime | Hourly consultancy, or as part of a maintenance contract | No. The [Registered Horticulturist](https://aih.org.au/registered-horticulturist-2/) credential is voluntary and peer-reviewed |

The practical consequence: **three of these four titles can be used by anyone,
today, with no training.** A licence check will not save you. What saves you is
asking to see three completed Perth jobs and one full documentation set.

## What they charge in Perth

Fee *structure* tells you more than fee level.

- **Design is sold in stages.** Concept, then developed design, then
  documentation. Perth designers commonly quote roughly $1,500–$2,500 for a
  basic concept plan and $5,000–$8,000 for a full documentation package
  ([indicative Perth figures](https://landscaping-perth.com.au/cost/landscaping-cost)
  from a referral site, not an independent authority — sanity-check, do not
  quote them at anyone).
- **A rule of thumb I use:** a design package you can actually build from tends
  to land somewhere between 5 and 10 per cent of the build cost. Below about
  $1,500 you are buying a picture, not a document, and the difference shows up
  as variations during the build.
- **Landscapers price two ways.** Fixed price against a defined scope, or day
  rate plus materials. Fixed price is only meaningful if the scope is
  documented — which is why Phase 6 exists.
- **Horticultural advice is cheap and undervalued.** An hour of consultancy
  before you order plants is the highest-return money in the project.

Get three written quotes on an identical scope. Consumer Protection lists what
a quote must contain — [contact details, ABN, licence number, description of
work, material prices, labour, timeframe, call-out fees and
terms](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/hiring-tradespeople-0).

## The work you may not do yourself, at any skill level

Read this section twice.

**Electrical.** In WA all electrical work must be done by a person holding the
relevant electrical licence, and it is illegal for an unlicensed person to carry
out electrical work including modifications to fixed wiring. That covers garden
lighting on mains, a shed sub-board, pump and pond circuits, and anything
feeding a water feature. Electricity plus wet sand plus bare feet is the
combination that kills people in gardens. Your electrical contractor must give
you an [electrical safety
certificate](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/multi-step-guides/electrical-safety-home/having-electrical-work-done)
within 28 days — collect it and file it. Check the licence prefix: EC is a
contractor, EW is an electrician.

**Plumbing.** Potable water pipework from the meter assembly to the points of
use is licensed plumbing work, as is sanitary work and underground drainage to
sewer. A Kalgoorlie handyman was fined $1,500 plus $571 costs in May 2026 for
exactly this. Where your retic ties into the supply — a new tap point, a cut
into the supply line, a backflow device — ring Building and Energy on
1300 489 099 before you start rather than guessing.

**Gasfitting.** Regulated under the Gas Standards Act 1972. Relevant the moment
your outdoor kitchen, fire pit or BBQ involves a fixed gas connection.

Verify any of the three in two minutes with the government's [licence
search](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/business-support/professional-accreditation/find-licensed-electrician-gas-fitter-or-plumber).

## Where builder registration starts

Landscaping is mostly unregistered work. It stops being unregistered when you
build something that needs a building permit and the value crosses a threshold.

| Situation | What is required |
| --- | --- |
| Permitted building work valued $20,000 or more | A registered building contractor, or owner-builder approval |
| Class 10a building (shed, carport, private garage) | Threshold is now $50,000 — raised from $20,000 on 1 July 2026 |
| Any residential building work over $20,000 | Home indemnity insurance |
| Fixed-price contract $7,500–$500,000 | Must be in writing and signed; deposit capped at 6.5% |
| Pool or spa holding water deeper than 30 cm | Building approval and a compliant barrier, re-inspected by council at least every four years |

The 1 July 2026 Class 10a change is recent and widely misquoted. A $35,000
carport that would have needed a registered builder in June 2026 does not need
one now — but it still needs a **building permit**, and permits are unaffected.
See [structures and building permits](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/).

Owner-builder approval has a sting: you generally cannot have held one in the
previous six years, and selling within seven years of the permit without home
indemnity insurance is an offence carrying a $10,000 penalty.

## What a professional does at this stage

A good Perth landscaper does three things before quoting, and they are all
about role boundaries.

1. **They decide which hat they are wearing, and price it separately.** Design
   fee and build price are different transactions. A "free design" is a sales
   tool, and it buys you a plan you cannot take to another builder.
2. **They subcontract the licensed trades and keep the paperwork.** Not "my
   mate does the sparky work". The electrical safety certificate, the plumbing
   compliance, the building permit and the home indemnity certificate go in a
   folder, because that folder is what you hand over at completion and what a
   buyer's conveyancer asks for in seven years.
3. **They price the hazard, not just the task.** Cutting pavers, limestone and
   concrete releases [respirable crystalline
   silica](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/silica) — cut wet, and wear a
   properly fitted P2. Trenches collapse. Working alone on a 40-degree February
   day is a genuine risk, not toughness.

## When to stop and hire someone

Write your own line now, before you are emotionally invested. Mine, for a
suburban Perth renovation, is a hard stop at any of these:

- **Anything electrical, plumbing or gas.** No exceptions, no skill level.
- **A retaining wall over about 500 mm, or any wall supporting a neighbour's
  soil, a driveway or a structure.** Engineer, then council — see
  [retaining walls](/curriculum/approvals/retaining-walls-approval/).
- **Permitted building work over the thresholds above**, unless you take on
  owner-builder approval with eyes open.
- **Anything touching a pool barrier.** A raised garden bed beside a pool fence
  can break compliance — see [pools and pool
  fencing](/curriculum/approvals/pools-and-pool-fencing/).
- **Tree work above head height, or near power lines.** Arborist.
- **Asbestos** in a pre-1990 fence or shed.
- **Machinery you have not operated before**, on a block with services in it.

Everything else on a normal Perth block is legally yours to do. The question
becomes economic rather than legal, and
[DIY vs subcontract](/curriculum/costing/diy-vs-subcontract/) is where you
answer it with numbers.
