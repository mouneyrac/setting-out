Lawn is the cheapest square metre in the garden to install and the most expensive
one to keep. On the Swan Coastal Plain that trade is sharper than almost anywhere
else in the country: the water drains straight through, the nutrients follow it
down, and a lawn laid on unamended grey sand is one you will be nursing for its
entire life.

So this lesson has two halves. Choose the species that matches the shade, wear
and water reality of your block rather than the one on the sample board. Then do
every piece of hard work *before* the turf arrives — because from the moment a
pallet lands on your driveway you are on a clock measured in hours. If you are
renovating, there is a third job at the front of the queue: killing the old lawn
properly. Kikuyu and couch do not die because you covered them up.

## What a professional does at this stage

A landscaper treats delivery day as a fixed deadline and works backwards from it.
Turf is a harvested crop with a shelf life, not a building material, and every
other decision bends to that.

The sequence is:

1. Levels set, subgrade compacted, falls confirmed away from the house
2. Soil profile built and amended to depth
3. Irrigation installed, pressure-tested and every head set to finish flush with
   the *final* turf surface
4. Wetting agent and starter fertiliser in, watered through
5. Area raked to a true final level and lightly firmed
6. Turf ordered for a nominated morning
7. Laid, rolled and heavily watered the same day
8. Exemption lodged that afternoon

Notice that turf is ordered at step six, not step one. The most common amateur
failure is buying turf first and then discovering the soil prep needs another two
days. They also order tight: measure properly, add 5 to 8 per cent for cuts around
curves and edges, and stop there. Leftover turf is money that rots on the verge.

## Choosing the species

Four species dominate Perth lawns. The
[Turf Growers Association of WA](https://tgawa.com.au/turf-varieties/) publishes
minimum sunlight and invasiveness ratings for each, which are the two numbers
that decide it.

| Species | Minimum direct sun | Wear and recovery | Thatch | Invasiveness | Where it earns its place |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soft leaf buffalo** (Sir Walter, Palmetto, Neergabby) | 4 hours | Moderate to good, self-repairing | Heavy — builds a spongy layer within a few years | Low to medium | The only realistic choice under trees, beside a two-storey wall, or in a narrow side-shaded yard |
| **Couch** (Wintergreen, Santa Ana, Plateau) | 5 hours | Excellent wear and green-up | Moderate, needs periodic renovation | Invasive to garden beds | Open, full-sun lawns wanting a fine, dense, sporting-field finish |
| **Kikuyu** (Village Green, Zulu, Eureka) | 5 to 6 hours | Excellent wear, fastest recovery of the four | Heavy and fast | **Very invasive to garden beds** | High-traffic family lawns and dogs, where cheapness and recovery beat neatness |
| **Zoysia** (Empire, Sir Grange) | 5 to 6 hours | Moderate to good, self-repairing but slow to recover | Builds slowly but is dense when it does | Low | Low-traffic feature lawns where you want a manicured look and less mowing |

Three things that table will not tell you:

- **Shade is measured in winter, not summer.** A patch getting six hours in
  January can get two in June behind a neighbour's roofline. Use the worst month
  from your [sun, shade and aspect](/curriculum/site-analysis/sun-shade-and-aspect/)
  mapping. Turf short of light does not die dramatically — it thins, weeds fill the
  gaps, and you blame the water.
- **Invasiveness is a maintenance cost you pay forever.** Kikuyu will get into
  your beds, through your dripline and under your paving. If you choose it, budget
  for a real edge — a hard mowing strip, not a plastic strip — in
  [mulch, edging and finishing](/curriculum/build/mulch-edging-and-finishing/).
- **Thatch is why old Perth lawns go water-repellent.** Buffalo and kikuyu build a
  dense layer of stems above the soil that sheds water sideways instead of letting
  it in. That is a scheduled renovation every few years, not a fault.

If more than about a third of your intended lawn fails the minimum sunlight test,
it is not a lawn. Make it a shade bed.

## Renovating: killing the old lawn first

Kikuyu and couch regrow from stolons above ground and runners buried in the sand.
Scalping, covering with soil, or laying new turf over the old all fail — the
previous lawn comes up through the new one within a season and you cannot
selectively remove it.

- **Spray, wait, spray again.** A registered glyphosate product on actively
  growing green leaf, full brown-off over 10 to 14 days, then a second pass on the
  regrowth. In Australia the approved label is the document that governs use — confirm
  the product is registered and read its approved label on the APVMA's
  [public chemical registration register](https://portal.apvma.gov.au/pubcris),
  because that is where the rates, the protective equipment and the interval before
  you can plant are set.
- **Cut it out.** Hire a turf cutter, remove the top 50 mm and take it away. No
  chemical, but you have now removed soil volume you must replace, and it is heavy.

For kikuyu, do both — spray first, then cut out the dead mat.

## Preparation: everything before the truck

**Set your finished level first.** Turf with its soil backing sits roughly 25 to
30 mm proud of the ground you prepare, so the prepared surface should finish about
25 to 30 mm *below* adjoining paving, edging and paths, and the mown lawn then
ends flush. Get this wrong and you have either a tripping lip or a strip the mower
scalps every pass.

**Build the profile.** Water Corporation's
[establishing a new lawn](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/lawn-care/establishing-a-new-lawn)
guidance gives the spec by sand type: on grey sands, remove the top 100 to 150 mm
and replace it with a quality 70 per cent red or yellow sand and 30 per cent
organic blend; on yellow sands, incorporate organics into the top 100 to 150 mm;
elsewhere on the coastal plain, work loam in to at least 100 to 150 mm and up to
300 mm. Same work as
[turning Perth sand into soil](/curriculum/build/turning-perth-sand-into-soil/) —
and it is the line item separating an
[$18 per square metre lawn from a $55 one](https://www.wcturf.com.au/cost-to-install-roll-on-lawn).

**Wetting agent and starter fertiliser go in before the turf.** Once turf is down
you cannot get either into the sand underneath, which is exactly where the roots
are heading.

**Finish the irrigation.** Every head set to the final turf level, and the station
uniform — a dry corner in a new lawn is dead within a fortnight because there is no
root system to ride it out. Do the catch-cup check in
[irrigation scheduling and uniformity](/curriculum/build/irrigation-scheduling-and-uniformity/)
*before* the turf covers your sprinklers.

**Rake, firm, rake.** Any hollow you leave shows for the life of the lawn.

## Delivery day

Turf is cut, rolled and stacked. Inside a stacked pallet it begins to heat and
compost. In a Perth summer, turf left on a pallet in the sun will yellow, then
die from the middle of the stack outward, and it can happen inside 24 hours.

- Order for delivery on the **morning you will lay it**. Never the afternoon
  before.
- If there is any delay, get the pallet into shade and keep it damp. Do not stack
  it on hot paving or bitumen.
- Lay the same day, full stop. Above about 35 degrees, treat it as a few hours.
- Start along the longest straight edge and work off planks, not the fresh turf.
- Stagger the joints in a brick bond, butt rolls tight without stretching them,
  and cut with a sharp knife or a spade against a board.
- Never finish with a strip narrower than about 300 mm — it dries out and dies.
  Shift the whole row instead.

## Rolling and the first watering

Roll or firm the laid turf. The purpose is to press the backing into contact with
the sand and eliminate air pockets, not to compact the soil — a lightly
water-filled lawn roller, or a plank and your body weight on a small area, is
plenty.

Then water immediately and heavily. Not a sprinkle: you want the turf wet through
and the sand beneath it wet to about 100 mm. Lift a corner an hour later, and if
the underside or the sand below is dry, keep going.

Water Corporation's establishment programme is three waterings a day for the first
week, tapering over the following weeks. Shallow roots develop in about two weeks;
do not mow until the turf resists a gentle tug, then mow high with sharp blades.

## The watering exemption: apply the same day

This is the legal part, and where people get fined.

Outside an exemption, Perth and Mandurah run a
[two-day sprinkler roster](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/check-your-watering-days),
once per day, before 9am or after 6pm, with $100 on-the-spot fines, plus a total
[Sprinkler Switch-off from 1 June to 31 August](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/sprinkler-switch-off).
Bore users are on the same roster and the same switch-off. New turf cannot
establish on that, and watering more often without an
[exemption](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/exemptions-and-breaches/watering-exemption/apply-for-a-watering-exemption)
is a breach.

| Installed | Exemption length | Daily target | Programme |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 October – 31 March | Up to **42 days** from the day of planting | 10 mm/day | Days 1–10: three times daily (3.5 mm each). Days 11–20: twice daily (5 mm each). Days 21–28: once daily. Days 29–42: every second day |
| 1 April – 30 September | Up to **35 days** from the day of planting | 7.5 mm/day | Days 1–14: twice daily (4 mm each). Days 15–28: once daily. Days 29–35: every second day |

Four things that catch people out:

1. **The clock starts at planting, not at approval.** Apply on the day you lay it
   or you lose days off the front.
2. **It covers roll-on lawn, runners, shredded lawn and seed**, and both manual
   and automatic irrigation qualify.
3. **Breaching the conditions is a $500 fine**, and Water Corporation holds the
   property owner responsible regardless of who installed the lawn or the retic.
4. **If you have a licensed bore, Water Corporation does not grant the exemption**
   — the Department of Water and Environmental Regulation manages those. Sort this
   out before you order turf, not after.

## The safety part

Turf day is the hottest and heaviest day of the build: wet, awkward rolls, in full
sun, under time pressure, usually in summer. WorkSafe WA's
[hot conditions guidance](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/working-safely-hot-conditions)
grades heat illness from discomfort through heat exhaustion (body temperature up
to 39°C) to heat stroke above 40°C, which is life-threatening. Its practical
control is 250 ml of water every 15 to 20 minutes, shaded rest breaks, and moving
hot tasks to the cooler part of the day.

Start at first light. Get help for the lifting. And note that the moment you pay
someone to help carry rolls, those duties are legally yours.
