[The last lesson](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydraulics/) gave you the physics.
This one gives you the four numbers you feed into it. Every decision from here —
how many sprinklers fit on a station, what size the mainline is, whether the
design fits inside your legal watering window — comes from measurements you take
this afternoon with a pressure gauge, a bucket and a stopwatch.

The temptation is to skip it. Most retic on this coastal plain was installed by
someone who assumed Perth mains water is plentiful. If your existing system mists
in the middle and dribbles at the end, you are looking at the result.

Two things before you start. First, if you are renovating you may not know what
your garden actually runs on — roughly one in four Perth households water from a
garden bore, and plenty of owners have never been told. Second, everything in
this lesson is measurement, which you may do yourself, but two jobs it points at
are licensed trade work in WA and always will be: the connection to scheme water
including the backflow device is a plumber's, and anything touching bore pump
wiring is a licensed electrical contractor's.

## What a professional does at this stage

An irrigation designer will not quote a system off an address. They turn up with
a gauge and measure, because the supplier's published numbers are useless as a
design input.

Water Corporation's commitment is [20 litres per minute at your meter](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/non-standard-water-service/water-conditions/flow-rate),
and its licensed pressure band in the metro area is
[15 to 100 metres head](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/water-issues/water-pressure/changes-in-water-pressure) —
roughly 150 to 1,000 kPa. Design to the bottom of that and you build a trickle
system; design to the top and you build one that fails every February.

The second thing a professional does is measure at the right time. Water
Corporation states that pressure drops during peak demand between 6am and 9am and
between 6pm and 9pm. Those are precisely the hours your retic is legally allowed
to run. A reading taken at 2pm on a Sunday measures water you will never be
permitted to use.

## The four measurements

| Measurement | How | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Static pressure** | Gauge on the tap nearest the meter, everything else off | Which nozzles and emitters can work at all |
| **Flow rate** | Timed bucket test, repeated three times | How many heads fit on one station |
| **Working pressure** | Gauge reading while that flow is running | Your real headroom, and pipe sizing |
| **Service and meter size** | Read the meter body and the service pipe | The ceiling nothing downstream can beat |

### Static pressure

Buy a hose-thread pressure gauge — any retic shop carries them. Screw it onto the
tap closest to the meter, turn everything else in the house off, open the tap
fully and read it. Take the reading twice, once at about 6.30pm and once at about
6.30am, and design to the lower one.

Two Perth-specific corrections. Every metre your garden sits above the meter costs
about 10 kPa, which matters on a stepped block or in the foothills. And if your
meter was recently replaced your pressure may have *dropped* — Water Corporation
notes that an old meter with a failed internal valve or filter was sometimes
flattering you.

### Flow rate

Use a bucket you have actually measured; nominal 9-litre buckets are routinely not
9 litres. Open the tap fully, time the fill with a stopwatch, and repeat three
times. Discard any outlier, average the rest, then:

**litres per minute = litres ÷ seconds × 60**

Cross-check it against Water Corporation's own method: everything off, read the
meter, run the tap for exactly 60 seconds, read the meter again — on a mechanical
meter the red digits are litres. Newer digital meters display live litres per
hour, which beats both. If the methods disagree badly, request a free pressure and
flow test at your meter through the
[low water pressure page](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/water-issues/water-pressure/low-water-pressure).

### Working pressure

This is the measurement almost every DIY system skips, and the one that explains
its failures. Static pressure is water standing still. The moment water moves,
friction in the
service, the meter, the backflow device and your own pipework eats into it. Read
the gauge *while* the design flow is running — a gauge with a pass-through hose
outlet is the easy way, otherwise gauge one tap while another runs at a measured
rate.

The gap between the two is your loss before the mainline even begins: static
480 kPa falling to 300 kPa at 25 litres per minute means 180 kPa has already gone.
Nozzle charts assume pressure at the head, not at the meter.

### Set a design flow, not a maximum

Never design to the number you measured. That was a good day, on a system a year
younger than it will be, with nobody else in the house using water. The working
rule in the trade is to design each station to no more than about three-quarters
of your measured available flow. Write that reduced figure into your workbook and
label it clearly — it is the number
[Lesson 3](/curriculum/build/irrigation-hydrozoning-and-stations/) tests every
station against.

### Service and meter size

Open the meter box and note the size stamped on the meter body, plus the size and
material of the service pipe to the house. Nothing downstream can outperform that.
On older Perth homes watch for galvanised service pipe: it corrodes inward, and a
nominal 20 mm pipe laid in 1965 may have half its bore left.

Water Corporation's [Backflow Prevention Policy](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/developing-and-building/building/backflow-prevention)
requires an approved backflow prevention containment device at the boundary of
every water service connection. Retic sits in fertilised soil and dog waste; the
device is what stops that reaching the main.

## If you have a bore — or might have one

**Find out.** Turn the scheme water off at the meter, then start the controller.
If the sprinklers still run, you have a bore. Power cables running from the
irrigation box into the ground point the same way. Add the answer to your
[existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/).

You will have one of two types. An older **well bore** has a metal cone-like lid,
a suction pipe with a foot valve, a surface pump and often a manual on/off switch
— which is exactly how gardens get left running for two days. A newer
**submersible bore** sits in a flush in-ground box with the pump down the hole,
and is more energy efficient.

Record these, and keep them with your house plans:

- Bore depth and the aquifer it draws from
- Pump make, model and rating, and whether it is submersible or surface
- Measured output — run the same timed bucket test at the bore outlet
- Standing water level, and salinity and pH from a water sample
- Any bore log from the driller

### The three legal points that catch people

**The roster applies to bores.** Garden bore users in Perth and Mandurah have the
same two rostered days, the same before-9am-or-after-6pm window, and the same
$100 fine. Water Corporation inspectors refer bore breaches to the Department of
Water and Environmental Regulation, which issues the infringement.

**The winter switch-off applies to bores.** No sprinklers from 1 June to 31 August.
You may run the system to the minimum extent necessary to keep the pump
serviceable, and testing is capped at two minutes per station.

**Licensing.** No licence is needed to take groundwater from the watertable
aquifer for domestic use or to water less than 0.2 hectares. At 2,000 square
metres or more, or any commercial use, you need a licence from DWER. Check the
[Perth Groundwater Map](https://maps.water.wa.gov.au/#/webmap/gwm) for your
maximum unlicensed bore depth before anyone drills.

### Safety and quality

Bore pump wiring is electrical work around water. Engage a licensed electrical
contractor and confirm the circuit is RCD protected — a WA home must have at least
two RCDs, and only a licensed contractor may install or repair them. Do not lift or
re-wire a submersible pump on your own on a Sunday afternoon.

Test the water before you design around it. Iron shows up as orange staining on
limestone and paving and progressively blocks drip emitters, which can rule out
dripline without treatment. Salinity constrains your plant palette harder than
soil type does. Both feed straight into
[emitter selection](/curriculum/build/irrigation-emitters/).

### The direction of travel

Perth's groundwater is being wound back, not expanded. The bore roster was cut
from three days to two on 1 September 2022, and under the state's
[rebalancing programme](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/natural-resources/water-resources/rebalancing-our-groundwater)
licensed entitlements fall 10 per cent and Water Corporation abstraction falls
27 per cent from 1 July 2028. In May 2026 Water Corporation asked households to
switch off a week early. Do not design a garden that only survives if the rules
loosen.
