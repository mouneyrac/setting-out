Demolition is the first day the block actually changes, and the stage where a
renovation budget quietly doubles. Three mistakes do it: putting everything in
one bin, starting the herbicide programme too late, and disturbing something that
contains asbestos.

The first costs you money. The second costs you the job twelve months later, when
kikuyu comes up through the joints of your new paving. The third can kill you,
slowly, decades from now. This lesson is the order of operations for removing
what your [garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/)
condemned.

## Asbestos: assume it is there until a laboratory says otherwise

Read this before you touch anything on a Perth block built before about 1990.

Asbestos cement is everywhere in older Perth back gardens: super six corrugated
fencing, fibro shed and garage walls, patio and carport roofing, eaves sheeting,
the flat sheet lining an old laundry, buried AC stormwater and sewer pipe, and
offcuts a previous owner used as packing under paving or as bed edging. That last
one catches people constantly, because it is not a structure and nobody looks for
it.

[Non-friable](https://www.worksafe.wa.gov.au/asbestos-safety-basics) asbestos is
bound in cement or resin and only releases fibres when that bond is damaged —
exactly what a grinder, demolition saw, hammer or pressure washer does. Friable
asbestos crushes to powder in the hand and is far more dangerous. The only way to
know which you have is a sample tested by a NATA accredited laboratory. Guessing
from the look of a sheet is not identification.

The legal position, from the regulator: a Class A or Class B licence is required
to remove **more than 10 square metres of non-friable** asbestos-containing
material at a workplace, and a Class A licence for **any amount of friable**
material. Anyone you pay to work on your block is at a workplace, so a
landscaper, handyman or bin company cannot legally take that fence out unless
they hold the licence. Ask to see it and check it against the WorkSafe register.

Doing it unpaid on your own home puts you in a different legal box but the same
physical one. If you proceed,
[follow the health department's method exactly](https://www.healthywa.wa.gov.au/Articles/A_E/Asbestos-removal):
mist the sheet with water or PVA first, hand tools only, never a power tool,
never a high-pressure hose, never break sheets to make them fit, P2 respirator
and disposable coveralls, and double-bag in polyethylene at least 0.2 mm thick
labelled DANGER ASBESTOS WASTE. It then goes to a landfill licensed by DWER —
Red Hill wants bundles of no more than three sheets if you unload by hand,
labelled CAUTION ASBESTOS in letters at least 50 mm high, and
**council tip passes are not valid for asbestos**.

For a full super six fence or a fibro shed, pay a licensed removalist. Removal,
wrapping, transport and disposal come as one price with a paper trail, and the
paper trail matters when you sell.

## The other hazards, briefly and seriously

- **Silica dust.** Cutting or breaking concrete, pavers, bricks, tiles or
  limestone releases respirable crystalline silica — concrete is under 30 per
  cent silica, brick 5 to 15 per cent. Wet-cut or use on-tool extraction, and
  treat a P2 mask as the last layer, not the first. Silicosis does not get
  better.
- **Trenches and undermining.** Never work in an unsupported excavation deeper
  than you are, and never undercut a wall or a fence footing to get a machine
  past.
- **Old power.** Unpermitted circuits to sheds, pool pumps and garden lights are
  common on older blocks and appear on no plan. See
  [locating services](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/) and lodge a
  free [BYDA](https://www.byda.com.au/) request before machinery arrives.
- **Heat.** Demolition in a Perth January is a genuine hazard. Start at first
  light, finish by lunch, do not work alone.

Demolishing a building rather than a garden also needs approval:
[a BA5 demolition permit](https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/building-and-energy/building-approvals)
from your local government, listed at $121.00 for a Class 1 or Class 10 structure
plus the Building Services Levy. Confirm the requirement and the current fee with
your council's building surveyor, and see
[structures and building permits](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/).

## Separate the streams before the first thing moves

Mixed waste is charged at the highest rate in the load. One wheelbarrow of soil
in a clean brick bin reclassifies the whole bin. That is the entire economics of
demolition, and why professionals sort at the source rather than later.

Run six physically separated piles from the first hour: **clean fill** (sand and
soil, no roots or rubbish), **green waste**, **inert rubble** (brick, concrete,
limestone, no timber or plastic), **metal**, **general waste**, and **asbestos**,
isolated and wrapped. Then choose a destination per stream, because gate rates
vary enormously between Perth facilities:

| Stream | Red Hill (EMRC), 2026/27 | Tamala Park (MRC), 2026/27 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General / mixed waste | $236.00 per tonne | $291.50 per tonne |
| Clean garden organics | $65.00 per tonne member resident, $98.00 commercial | no separate rate — $291.50 per tonne |
| Wrapped asbestos | $126.00 per tonne member resident, $330.00 commercial | $450.00 per tonne |

Green waste is roughly three and a half times cheaper per tonne at
[Red Hill](https://www.emrc.org.au/our-services-and-products/our-services/fees-and-charges.aspx)
than at [Tamala Park](https://mrc.wa.gov.au/tamala-park/disposal/fees-charges.aspx),
which for a northern suburbs job usually means sending clean organics to a
commercial composter instead. Check your own regional council's schedule; these
change every 1 July.

Before anything leaves site, decide what stays. Clean limestone rubble crushes
into base material. Sound bricks and pavers are worth real money and are often
the exact match you will need for a repair later. Clean sand is fill you would
otherwise buy back. Stockpile it clear of the works and carry the volumes into
your [quantity take-off](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/).

## Killing kikuyu and couch — and how long it really takes

This is the section people skip and then pay for.

Kikuyu and couch both spread by **stolons** above ground and **rhizomes** below
it, with every node capable of producing a new plant. That single fact drives
everything: a rotary hoe or a bobcat bucket does not remove the grass, it
multiplies it and spreads the pieces evenly across your site. The UC IPM notes
put it plainly — cultivation chops the stems into segments, some of those
segments root and become new plants, and hoeing transplants rhizomes, especially
if watering follows.

So the sequence is the reverse of what feels natural. **Kill it first. Dig
second.**

1. **Get it growing.** Drought-stressed grass barely takes up herbicide. Water
   it, mow it, let it come back to full soft leaf. Two to three weeks.
2. **Spray while it is actively growing**, at the label rate for the product you
   bought. Do not mow, dig or cultivate for at least seven days after.
3. **Wait for the browning, then wait longer.** Two to three weeks; deeper
   rhizomes will push new shoots.
4. **Spray the regrowth.** Assume two applications, plan for three. UC IPM's
   figure for kikuyu is three to four, timed to whenever new growth appears.
5. **Verify before you build.** Turn over a spade of soil in three places and
   look for white rhizomes with live green nodes. One live node under a paving
   base is a future repair.

Realistically that is **six to ten weeks minimum**, and on established kikuyu it
can be a full season. In Perth the effective window is roughly October to April,
when the soil is warm and the grass is genuinely growing. Winter spraying is slow
because the plant is barely moving, and from **1 June to 31 August the
[sprinkler switch-off](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/winter-sprinkler-switch-off)**
means you cannot run retic to push growth — hand watering is your only lever, and
the $100 fine applies to bore users too.

If you will not use herbicide, solarisation under clear plastic needs four to six
weeks of peak heat, December to February here, sealed at the edges and extended
well past the visible edge of the grass. Smothering under light-proof material
takes six to twelve months. Neither is a weekend.

Two things that never work: weed mat over live kikuyu, and paving over live
couch. Both come through.

## What a professional does at this stage

Three things, none of them physical.

**They price the demolition by stream and by tonne before a tool is picked up.**
Skip volumes, tip runs, salvage credits and the asbestos line are quoted
separately, because that is the only way a variation can later be argued.

**They start the herbicide programme weeks before the machinery is booked.**
Spraying is the longest-lead item in the whole build and costs almost nothing.
Sequencing it late is the most common failure in a Perth renovation, and it stays
invisible until the new lawn or paving is already down.

**They do not clear first and set out afterwards.** Demolition destroys your
reference points, so levels, boundary offsets and datum are established and
protected before anything is removed. Nothing recovers a datum peg a bobcat has
buried. Photograph the site before, during and after — an exposed subgrade or a
service run you uncovered is worth far more as a photograph than as a note, and
you will want it for your
[as-built drawings](/curriculum/handover/as-built-drawings/).

## What happens next

You should now have a stripped site, sorted and costed waste, a stockpile of
salvage, and a perennial grass either dead or on a documented spray programme
with a date it will be clear.
