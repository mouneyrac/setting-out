A deck is the only thing in this course that is unambiguously a building. It has
footings, a structural frame, a load path and a code that governs it, and in
Western Australia it needs a building permit. Treat it as landscaping and you
will build something that is unapprovable, uninsurable and — the part people
underestimate — a covered, ventilated, timber-lined highway from the sand into
your wall frame.

Most Perth decks are renovation work: over tired paving, beside an existing
alfresco, or replacing a twenty-year-old jarrah deck that has gone soft at the
posts. That changes the first question. Before you design anything, find out
what is already against that wall — the damp-proof course, the weepholes, the
slab edge, and whatever termite treatment the house was built with.

**Safety first, plainly.** Cutting a deck's worth of hardwood produces a lot of
jarrah dust, and hardwood dust is a recognised carcinogen, not a nuisance —
extract at the tool and wear P2. Drilling masonry for a ledger produces
crystalline silica; drill wet or on extraction. If your house is pre-1990 and
you are removing eaves or cladding to fit that ledger, assume asbestos until
proven otherwise and read
[auditing an existing garden](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/)
again. And do not work alone on a raised frame in a Perth February.

## What a professional does at this stage

Four decisions, in this order, before any timber is ordered.

**One: free-standing or attached.** The default professional answer is
free-standing, on its own posts, not connected to the house. Fixing a deck to a
building is the most common single cause of deck failure in Australia. If you
attach it, you are into NCC Housing Provisions
[Part 12.3](https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/editions/ncc-2025/adopted/housing-provisions/12-ancillary-provisions/part-123-attachment-framed-decks-and-balconies-external-walls-buildings-using)
— a waling plate of a specified size and stress grade, two No. 14 screws at no
more than 450 mm centres into a timber-framed wall embedded at least 44 mm,
staggered fixings at 300 mm centres into masonry, flashing extending 150 mm
beyond each side of the plate, and strap bracing once the deck is over 1 m up.

**Two: the finished deck height.** Set it off the house, not off the existing
paving. The damp-proof course must stay at least 150 mm above finished ground,
or 75 mm above paving that falls away from the wall, and the weepholes at 1.2 m
centres must stay clear. Perth renovations break this constantly by building a
deck up to the brickwork.

**Three: the board.** The board dictates joist spacing, joist spacing dictates
bearer spacing, and bearer spacing dictates where the footings go. You design
backwards from the board.

**Four: the permit.** Get it before you order timber, not after.

## Approvals: a deck is building work

[Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/law_s43955.html)
of the Building Regulations 2012 lists everything you can build in WA without a
permit. There is an item for a pergola up to 20 square metres and 2.4 m high,
one for a freestanding Class 10a building up to 10 square metres, one for a
retaining wall up to 0.5 m. There is no item for a deck.

In practice councils publish workable triggers. The City of Joondalup requires a
building permit once a deck exceeds 20 square metres, is higher than 500 mm,
comes within 900 mm of a boundary, sits in a bushfire prone area, or carries
privacy screens — and requires a 1.6 m privacy screen where the deck is within
7.5 m of a boundary. That is one council's application of state rules; get yours
in writing. Planning approval is a separate question again, driven by the
R-Codes — see [R-codes and your council](/curriculum/approvals/r-codes-and-your-council/)
and [structures and building permits](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/).

Two things changed recently and both matter. **From 1 July 2026** the registered
building contractor threshold rose to $50,000 for Class 10a work (it stays at
$20,000 for other building work). And Building and Energy separately lists
*installation of timber decking* among the work that does not require a
registered building contractor at all. Neither removes the permit. Also note WA
adopted **NCC 2025 on 1 May 2026** with a 12-month transition, so until 30 April
2027 your surveyor may be working to either edition.

## Footings, posts and bracing

The standard detail is a concrete footing with a galvanised stirrup, with the
post held **at least 75 mm above finished ground, paving or landscaping level**
so the base can be inspected, and a 5–25 mm gap under the post for ventilation.
Footings are designed to AS 2870 or AS 1684 from your soil classification —
which is why [Perth soils](/curriculum/site-analysis/perth-soils/) matters here,
and why sand over an old service trench is not the same site as clean Karrakatta
sand.

If you embed posts directly instead, use no-fines concrete, at least 150 mm of
concrete under the post, and slope the top away — and use in-ground durability
Class 1 timber or H5 treatment. H3-treated pine in the ground is a five-year
deck. Maximum post height for a given size is 15 times the post face width, and
once the deck surface is 1 m up it must be braced: 30 x 0.8 mm galvanised strap
at 30–60 degrees, runs no longer than 4 m.

Dig and pour the same day. Perth sand does not hold a clean hole overnight.
Check [locating services](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/) before
any of it.

## Spans: let the board choose the joists

| Decking board | Maximum joist spacing |
| --- | --- |
| 19 mm hardwood, medium feature or standard grade | 500 mm |
| 19 mm cypress | 400 mm |
| 21 mm cypress | 450 mm |
| 19 mm treated softwood | 400 mm |
| 22 mm treated softwood | 450 mm |
| 23 mm composite (typical Australian brand) | 450 mm |
| 32 mm composite (typical Australian brand) | 600 mm |

Timber figures are AS 1684 via WoodSolutions; composite figures are the
manufacturer's and vary by brand — use the sheet for the board you are actually
buying. Lay boards at 45 degrees and the spacing tightens: 19 mm hardwood needs
joists at 350 mm to keep within the 500 mm span.

Bearer and joist sizes come from AS 1684 — Tables 49 and 50 for decks more than
1000 mm above ground, Tables 5 and 6 for lower decks. Keep joists at least
45 mm wide (50 mm for unseasoned hardwood) so fixings do not split them; 35 mm
and 38 mm joists only work with side-fix clips. Join joists over a bearer with
at least 30 mm bearing. **None of these tables apply if you are tiling the deck
or putting a spa on it** — that is an engineer's job.

## Clearance, ventilation and drainage

Minimum ground clearance to the lowest framing member is 150 mm, rising to
**400 mm where a termite management system needs to be inspected**. Below
400 mm to the board surface you are building a close-to-ground deck, which needs
more: the ground cleared and graded away from the house, a plastic membrane over
compacted sand or gravel, the perimeter left open, and the deck panelised so
sections can be lifted for inspection.

Board gaps: 3–4 mm for 86–90 mm seasoned hardwood, 5–6 mm on close-to-ground
decks for airflow. Composite moves with heat rather than moisture — roughly 3 mm
over a 5 m length across a 40-degree swing — so honour the manufacturer's butt
and side gaps and fix when it is under 25 degrees and out of full sun.

The water that ruins Perth decks is not rain, it is roof and paving runoff
directed under the frame. Resolve it in
[drainage and soakwells](/curriculum/build/drainage-and-soakwells/) first.

## Termites: the part that costs you a house

Western Australia is treated as a termite risk area under the Building Code. A
deck's framing and boards are primary building elements. So you have three
compliant options: naturally termite-resistant timber under Appendix C of
AS 3660.1, preservative-treated timber under Appendix D, or a termite barrier
installed to AS 3660.1.

**Jarrah is not option one.** It was removed from the AS 3660.1 naturally
termite-resistant list in the 2014 edition. General timber references still
describe jarrah as termite resistant, and horticulturally that is fair, but for
compliance it is off the list. Do not let a jarrah deck be your termite strategy.

Where a barrier is your method, AS 3660.1 requires attachments such as decks to
be **at least 25 mm clear of the building**; if you cannot achieve that, the
barrier must be extended to cover the deck. Close to the ground, leave a
**40 mm gap** between the house and the deck for inspection and airflow, and
keep decking boards 10 mm clear of the wall. Screw rather than nail the first
two boards along the house so they lift for inspection without damage.

Then the Perth-specific trap. Where the soil around a susceptible building
becomes concealed or inaccessible — which is exactly what a deck against the
house does — a chemical termite system must be **replenishable via a
reticulation system**. Retro-fitting reticulation under a finished deck is
demolition. Decide this before the frame goes down, and make sure the durable
notice recording the system, install date, chemical life and inspection interval
ends up in the meter box.

## Jarrah, treated pine or composite

| | Above-ground durability | Real WA considerations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Jarrah** | Class 2 | Local, dense, BAL-rated by density. Not top of the durability table — blackbutt, spotted gum and merbau are Class 1. Commercial native forest logging ended in WA on 1 January 2024, so supply is now recycled, salvage or ecological thinning. Tannin bleeds and stains paving below. |
| **H3 treated pine** | H3 hazard level | The cheapest compliant framing and boards. H2, H2F and H2S are *not* suitable outdoors; LOSP is not suitable in-ground; CCA boards and handrails are not permitted where children have frequent contact. |
| **Composite** | n/a | No splinters, no oiling, consistent. But it runs roughly 20–25 degrees above ambient in full sun — a 40-degree February day is a 60-degree deck. West and north-facing, dark colours, bare feet: think hard. |

Durability classes describe heartwood only. Sapwood in every species is
non-durable outdoors.

## Fixings and the coast

Every framing bolt, screw, nail and bracket must be hot-dip galvanised or
stainless. Electroplated fixings break down early and are not acceptable.
**Within 1 km of the coast, or within 1 m of a pool edge, use stainless.** That
is most of the western suburbs, Scarborough, Hillarys, Mullaloo, Fremantle,
Cottesloe — and every pool deck in the metro area regardless of postcode. Fix
boards flush, never punched, and keep fixings 12 mm from ends and edges with
holes pre-drilled.

One detail that only shows up later: never cut masonry or metal above a laid
hardwood deck. Angle grinder filings react with tannin and leave permanent black
staining.

Finally, if any part of the deck allows a fall of 1 m or more, you need a
compliant barrier — 1 m high, no gap passing a 125 mm sphere, non-climbable
