Garden lighting is the last thing most people think about and one of the first
things that has to go in the ground. The cable goes in before the paving, the
soil, the mulch and the plants. Get that sequence wrong and your choices are a
trench through a finished garden or an extension lead across the lawn forever.

This lesson installs what you drew in the
[lighting plan](/curriculum/documentation/lighting-plan/). It is also the one part
of a landscape build with a criminal penalty attached, so we deal with that
first.

## The legal line in WA

Under regulation 4A of the [Electricity (Licensing) Regulations 1991](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/RedirectURL?OpenAgent=&query=mrdoc_46377.htm),
**electrical work** means work on installations, appliances or equipment supplied
at a nominal pressure **exceeding 50 volts a.c. or 120 volts ripple free d.c.**
Regulation 19 makes it an offence to carry out electrical work unless you hold a
licence or permit, and regulation 65 sets the general penalty at **$50,000 for an
individual**. Building and Energy states it without qualification: it is illegal
for an unlicensed person to carry out electrical work in WA.

That 50 volt figure draws a clean line through your garden.

| The work | Who may do it in WA |
| --- | --- |
| Installing or altering a fixed weatherproof outdoor power point for the transformer | Licensed electrical contractor only |
| Hard-wiring a transformer, or any 240 V bollard, floodlight, wall light or pergola downlight | Licensed electrical contractor only |
| Running a new circuit, or anything at the switchboard | Licensed electrical contractor only |
| Bonding metalwork within 1.25 m of a pool | Licensed electrical contractor only |
| Plugging an approved 12 V transformer into an existing outdoor power point | You |
| Cable, connectors and fixtures downstream of that transformer, at 12 V | You |

Two things follow. Check the licence before anyone opens your switchboard —
contractors are **EC**, electricians are **EW**, both searchable on the
[WA Online Licence Search](https://ols.demirs.wa.gov.au/search). And for any
electrical work other than maintenance or like-for-like replacement, your
contractor **must give you an electrical safety certificate within 28 days**.
That is not a receipt; it is the document you hand a buyer at settlement. Do not
pay the final invoice without it, and file it with your
[warranties and records](/curriculum/handover/warranties-and-records/).

The other hazard is what is already buried. Lodge a free
[Before You Dig Australia](https://www.byda.com.au/) enquiry days before you
trench — noting it shows registered network assets only, so the unmarked cable a
previous owner ran to the shed appears on no plan. Hand-dig near the meter box,
and re-read your
[locating services](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/) notes.

## What a professional does at this stage

They treat lighting the way an electrician treats a house: **first fix and second
fix, two separate visits.**

First fix is conduit, sleeves and cable, laid in the same trenching pass as the
[retic install](/curriculum/build/irrigation-install-and-audit/) and before
[base preparation](/curriculum/build/base-preparation/) closes anything in. Second
fix is fixtures, connections, transformer and commissioning — after planting, at
night.

The reasoning is economic. A conduit sleeved under a path during base preparation
costs ten dollars and five minutes. The same crossing retro-fitted under finished
liquid limestone is a saw cut, a patch that never matches, or a directional bore.
Hence the habits:

- **Sleeve under every hard surface**, draw cord left in, plus one spare sleeve
  you have no current use for.
- **Never share a trench with retic poly.** Offset 300 mm or run in conduit, or
  the shovel that repairs a solenoid in three years finds your cable.
- **Leave a 500 mm service loop** at every fixture and junction, so fittings can
  move once the garden grows in.
- **Photograph and dimension every run before backfilling.** Perth sand keeps no
  memory of a trench. Those photos become your
  [as-built drawings](/curriculum/handover/as-built-drawings/).
- **Book the electrician once**, for the outdoor GPO and any mains fittings
  together, and collect the certificate.
- **Commission at night**, aiming each fitting with the client standing there.

## Sizing the transformer

Add up the wattage of every fixture on the run. Then add headroom: keep the
connected load at or below **80% of the transformer's rating**. Twelve 4 W
fixtures is 48 W, which wants a 100 W transformer, not a 60 W one.

The headroom covers the fixtures you add later, keeps the unit off its thermal
limit through a Perth February, and leaves room to move to a higher voltage tap.

The trap in the other direction, on a renovation: older iron-core transformers
sized for halogen often specify a **minimum** load, and hanging 30 W of LED off a
300 VA halogen unit can leave the output unregulated and cook the fittings.
Converting halogen to LED means replacing the transformer too.

Mount it at least 300 mm above ground, in shade, out of sprinkler throw, where
you can read the label — not behind the future screening hedge. It plugs into a
weatherproof outdoor GPO that is RCD protected; WA homes must have at least two
RCDs covering all power and lighting circuits.

## Voltage drop over long runs

This decides whether the far end of the garden looks like the near end. Voltage
lost in the cable is roughly:

**volts dropped = 0.035 × length in metres × current in amps ÷ cable size in mm²**

Current is watts ÷ 12. Keep the drop under about **1.5 V** to the furthest
fixture; most 12 V LED garden fittings work somewhere in a 10–15 V band, but check
the datasheet for yours. The table is worst case, all load at the far end — with
fixtures spread evenly along a run you get roughly double these distances.

| Cable (twin) | 24 W (2 A) | 48 W (4 A) | 72 W (6 A) | 96 W (8 A) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 mm² | 30 m | 15 m | 10 m | 8 m |
| 2.5 mm² | 50 m | 25 m | 17 m | 13 m |
| 4 mm² | 80 m | 40 m | 27 m | 20 m |
| 6 mm² | 120 m | 60 m | 40 m | 30 m |

Worked example. Twelve 4 W fixtures is 48 W, so 4 A. The far bed is 30 metres
away. On 2.5 mm² you run out of budget at 25 m — so either step up to 4 mm², or
split the garden into two 24 W runs of 2 A each, which 2.5 mm² carries to 50 m.
Splitting is usually cheaper than upsizing.

Two other levers: a multi-tap transformer (12 V, 13 V, 14 V, 15 V outputs) lets
you push a long run harder without over-volting a short one, and a hub layout —
one heavy trunk to a buried junction, short spokes to fixtures — beats
daisy-chaining lights in series. Then verify: put a multimeter on the last fixture
of each run before you backfill. Measured, not assumed.

## Cable, joints and depth

Use double-insulated, UV-stable cable rated for direct burial — not figure-8
speaker wire, not household TPS. Joints are where these systems die: in Perth sand
wet from retic three mornings a week, a twisted joint wrapped in tape corrodes
green inside one summer. Use IP68 gel-filled or resin-filled direct-burial
connectors.

On depth, AS/NZS 3000 sets minimum depths of cover for underground wiring, and the
figure depends on the cable and whether it is in conduit — **ask your electrical
contractor for the number that applies rather than trusting a depth off a blog**.
In practice: deeper than feels necessary in open beds, and always in conduit under
paving, driveways and any lawn you might one day core.

## Fixtures for Perth conditions

- **IP rating.** IP65 above ground, IP67 set into the ground, IP68 in water.
  In-ground uplights fill with sand and leaf litter here — check the fitting
  drains, or spike-mount and hide it in planting.
- **Coastal metalwork.** From Mandurah to Hillarys, powder-coated aluminium and
  304 stainless pit and streak within a couple of seasons. Use solid brass, copper
  or 316 stainless. Brass patinas brown then green; that is the material working.
- **Bore water.** Iron stains brass, stainless and glass lenses orange. Aim
  sprinklers off the fixtures, or budget an annual clean.
- **Colour temperature.** 2700 K, 3000 K at most. Cooler light reads as
  institutional, attracts more insects, and the
  [national light pollution guidelines](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/publications/national-light-pollution-guidelines-wildlife)
  are explicit that cutting blue content matters for wildlife.
- **Limestone reflects.** Limestone walls, liquid limestone and pale paving bounce
  far more light than dark materials. You need fewer lumens than the catalogue
  suggests.

## Aiming and shielding, after dark

Install the fixtures loose. Come back after sunset with the swivels still
adjustable, aim every one, then lock them off. Nobody has ever aimed a garden
light correctly in daylight.

1. **Stand where people stand.** Walk the path, sit in the alfresco chair, look
   out the kitchen window. If you see the lamp rather than the lit object, that is
   glare — recess it deeper in its cowl, add a hex louvre, or move it.
2. **Walk to the street and look back.** On a 400 m² Perth block your uplight is
   often six metres from a neighbour's bedroom window.
3. **Light the object, not the air.** Keep fittings low, directed and shielded so
   nothing spills above the horizontal.

Then set the timer for how the garden is used — sunset to about 11pm in summer —
not dusk to dawn.

## Renovating: what is already there

**Existing 240 V garden lighting with no paperwork.** Spike lights and floodlights
put in by a previous owner may or may not have been done by a licensed contractor,
and you cannot tell by looking. Have one inspect and either certify or remove them.
Until then it is an electrocution risk in a wet bed and a disclosure problem at
sale.

**Anything within 1.25 m of the pool.** Metal light fittings that close to the
water must be bonded to the main earthing system, along with metal fencing,
handrails and ladders — and only a licensed electrical contractor may do it. A
brass uplight beside pool coping is not a DIY job at any voltage. Read
[pools and pool fencing](/curriculum/approvals/pools-and-pool-fencing/) alongside
this, since raising a bed near a barrier can break compliance at the same time.

## What happens next

Cable and conduit are in, the transformer is sized with headroom, the mains work
is certified. The fixtures stay loose and unaimed until planting is done, because
you cannot aim a light at a plant that is not there yet.

lighting cable, already in the ground, disappears under it.
