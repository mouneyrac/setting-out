Drainage is the only part of a landscape you cannot go back and fix. Every other
mistake here is recoverable — the wrong plant comes out, the wrong paver gets
lifted. A soakwell in the wrong place, or a stormwater line with a sag in it, is
under a hundred square metres of paving by the time you find out.

So it goes in now, before base preparation, before retaining, before anything is
built on top of it. On a renovation there is a second problem: there is already
drainage down there. Old soakwells, abandoned brick soak pits, downpipes
discharging onto bare sand beside the footings. Some of it works. Some of it is a
void waiting to collapse under your new alfresco.

## Safety before anything else

Sand gives no warning before it collapses. WorkSafe WA's excavation code is
blunt: nobody should work in an excavation 1.5 metres or more deep that is not
protected by shoring. A 1200 mm soakwell hole in Perth sand, with a machine
beside it and a concrete ring swinging overhead, is close enough to that line
that you should not be standing in it. Work from the edge.

- **Services.** Lodge a free [Before You Dig
  Australia](https://www.byda.com.au/) enquiry before you touch the ground, then
  treat the plans as approximate — Water Corporation says potholing by hand or
  vacuum excavation is the only way to locate a pipeline with certainty. The
  excavation code is sharper still: hand dig from the side, and never use a
  machine to find a service. See
  [locating services](/curriculum/site-analysis/locating-services/).
- **Silica.** Cutting a concrete lid, a limestone block or a paver dry with an
  angle grinder is the highest-exposure thing you will do on this site. Wet cut
  or use on-tool extraction, plus a fitted P2. The WA exposure standard is
  0.05 mg per cubic metre over eight hours, and you will exceed it in minutes
  cutting dry.
- **Load.** A concrete soakwell ring is a back injury waiting to happen. Chain it
  to the machine.

## What a professional does at this stage

They set the drainage first and let everything else follow. An amateur designs
the paving and then works out where the water goes. A landscaper starts at the
lowest fixed point — the soakwell invert — and works backwards up the falls to
the finished surface levels. The other way round is how you end up with 20 mm of
fall across 9 metres and a puddle at the back door.

Three checks before the first hole:

1. **Your council's rule, in writing** — not a rule of thumb. Councils genuinely
   differ: Stirling sizes storage at impervious area times 0.015 (15 mm across
   the site), Cockburn uses V = A ÷ 80, or 12.5 mm, for a 1 in 20 year
   five-minute storm. On 200 square metres that is 3.0 cubic metres against 2.5 —
   one extra soakwell.
2. **The winter watertable.** Check the [Perth Groundwater
   Map](https://maps.water.wa.gov.au/#/webmap/gwm). A soakwell whose base sits
   below the maximum groundwater level fills in June and stays full. That is a
   tank, and the design has to change.
3. **What is under the hole.** Coffee rock under Bassendean sand and cemented
   limestone under Spearwood sand both stop infiltration dead, and your
   [Perth soils](/curriculum/site-analysis/perth-soils/) test holes already told
   you which you have.

## Sizing and placing the wells

Work from your [drainage plan](/curriculum/documentation/drainage-plan/). Measure
every new impervious surface, apply your council's factor, divide by the capacity
of the size you have chosen, round up. Capacities published by Stirling:

| Soakwell (diameter x depth) | Capacity |
| --- | --- |
| 900 x 600 mm | 0.38 m³ |
| 1200 x 900 mm | 1.02 m³ |
| 1200 x 1200 mm | 1.36 m³ |
| 1500 x 1200 mm | 2.10 m³ |
| 1800 x 1200 mm | 3.05 m³ |

Placement rules that catch people out:

- **Clearance.** At least 1 metre edge to edge from any structure or boundary in
  Stirling, or the manufacturer's spec if greater. Closer and you are soaking the
  footings you meant to protect.
- **Trafficable where it matters.** Under a driveway, carport or garage it must
  be traffic rated on an approved base.
- **Roof and surface water stay separate.** Stirling requires it, and surface
  runoff carries the silt that kills infiltration.
- **Access stays.** Raising levels means fitting a riser. Do not pave over a lid.

## Falls, and the sin of the belly

A belly is a sag that holds water. Silt drops out of standing water, builds, and
blocks the line — usually in the third or fourth winter, under the paving.
Bellies come from laying pipe on a lumpy trench bottom and letting the backfill do
the shaping. So shape the trench, not the pipe:

1. Trim the trench bottom to a continuous grade with a string line, working from
   the invert you set at the well.
2. Bed the full length on 50 mm of clean sand so the pipe is supported
   continuously, not spanning high spots.
3. Hold a minimum of 1 in 100 — 10 mm per metre. More fall is fine; inconsistent
   fall is not. Cockburn requires at least 100 mm diameter where wells are
   linked.
4. Check with a level or laser every 2 metres as you lay, not at the end.
5. Keep cover: 100 mm under soil and 50 mm under paving or concrete over a 90 mm
   UPVC drain, more under vehicles.

Surface falls are code too. The ground must fall away from the house 50 mm over
the first metre (25 mm in low-rainfall areas with impermeable surfaces). And in
Stirling, downpipes must discharge into a grated inlet before entering the
infiltration system — no hard connection into the well, which is just as well,
because the grate is your leaf and silt trap.

## Geotextile

Perth sand is fine and mobile. Geotextile stops it migrating into the voids you
have just paid to create. Use a non-woven needle-punched fabric — ask for bidim —
in three places:

- **Over the lid and joints of a concrete soakwell** before backfilling. Sand
  washes in through the ring joints, and a well half full of sand has half the
  capacity you sized. Leave the base open.
- **Wrapping the aggregate around a subsoil drain**: fabric, aggregate, pipe,
  fold over. Water through, fines stopped.
- **Between clean aggregate and surrounding sand** anywhere the two would mix.

Do not wrap it across a face you rely on for infiltration into silty material —
it will blind over.

## Subsoil drainage for the wet corner

A corner that stays damp all winter is perched water sitting on an impeding
layer, and surface drainage will not touch it. The answer is a socked slotted agi
line in a geotextile-wrapped trench of clean aggregate, at a uniform fall of not
less than 1:300, running to a soakwell, a sump, or a low point on your own land.
Never to the neighbour's: the NCC requires surface water from a 5% AEP storm to
be disposed of without nuisance to any other property, and Cockburn puts it
flatly — you confine stormwater within your boundaries.

Depth matters more than length. The drain must sit at or below the impeding
layer; a shallow agi line in sand does nothing. And if the corner is wet because
the watertable is high rather than perched, the drain has nowhere to discharge —
raise the levels or plant for it instead.

## Test with water before you backfill

The step people skip, and the cheapest insurance on the job. Trench open, every
joint visible:

1. **Run a hose** into each downpipe and grate for ten to fifteen minutes at real
   flow, and walk the line while it runs.
2. **Surface wetting** over the pipe means a joint that has not welded, or a
   rubber ring that has rolled.
3. **Water standing in the pipe** after the flow stops is a belly. Fix it now.
4. **Water backing up at a grate** means a blockage, a reverse fall or an
   undersized line.
5. **Soak-test a well.** Fill it and time the drop. Still full an hour later on a
   dry summer day means an impeding layer or the watertable, and the design
   changes before it is buried.
6. **Photograph everything** with a tape in frame against a fixed reference. That
   is your as-built, and in ten years it is the difference between a repair and
   an excavation.

Then backfill in 150 to 200 mm compacted layers, haunching the first layer around
the pipe by hand. Backfill a poly soakwell evenly on all sides at once — load one
side and it will distort or float.
