Planting is the cheapest day of the build and the most expensive one to repeat. The
plants might be five per cent of the job's cost. Replacing them all in eighteen
months costs you the plants again, plus the labour, plus a season of looking at a
garden that failed.

Almost every plant that dies in a new Perth garden dies for one of four reasons: it
went in at the wrong time of year, it went in too big, it went in with a root ball
already strangling itself, or nobody watered it properly through its first summer.
None of those are horticultural subtleties. All four are decided before the plant
leaves the nursery, or in the ten minutes it takes to put it in the ground.

Renovating adds a fifth. A new shrub 1.5 metres from an established lemon-scented gum
is not competing on level terms, and no amount of establishment watering fixes that.
Look at what is already in the ground before you dig.

## Where planting sits in the sequence

1. **Soil first**, whole beds rather than planting holes — [turning Perth sand into soil](/curriculum/build/turning-perth-sand-into-soil/).
2. **Irrigation installed, pressure-tested and running.** You cannot commission retic through a planted bed without walking on it — [irrigation install and audit](/curriculum/build/irrigation-install-and-audit/).
3. **Set out and plant.**
4. **Mulch after planting, never before** — [mulch, edging and finishing](/curriculum/build/mulch-edging-and-finishing/).
5. **Water in, and apply for the exemption the same day.**

Turf goes down after planting, so you are not dragging plants and soil across new
lawn.

## What a professional does at this stage

They do not go plant shopping. They order off the plant schedule from
[the planting plan](/curriculum/documentation/planting-plan-and-schedule/) — species,
size, count — six to twelve weeks ahead, because WA growers produce to order and the
interesting species sell out.

On site, they set every plant out in its pot, in position, and walk away for five
minutes before a hole is dug. Standing back is free. Moving a planted 45 litre tree
is not.

And they size down deliberately. Someone paid for a result rather than for an
impressive delivery photo buys the smallest stock that satisfies the design intent,
because smaller stock establishes faster, costs less, and is standing taller than the
big one three years later. Big stock is bought for one reason: structure the design
needs *now* — screening a neighbour's first-floor window, shading a west wall,
anchoring an entry.

## Autumn, and why it is not negotiable

Water Corporation is unambiguous:
[the best time to plant is late autumn or early winter](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/Help-and-advice/Waterwise/Garden/Creating-a-waterwise-verge),
because winter rainfall does the establishment work for you.

In April the sand still carries summer heat, so roots grow, while evaporative demand
has collapsed, so the plant is not losing water faster than it can take it up. Plant
in April and the root system has roughly seven months to spread before its first
February. Plant in October and it has about eight weeks.

| Window | Verdict | What it means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **April – May** | Best | Warm soil, mild air, rain arriving. Default choice for everything. |
| **June – August** | Good, especially for natives | Sprinklers are banned 1 June to 31 August unless you hold an exemption. Rain usually carries the plants anyway. |
| **September – October** | Acceptable | Only with working irrigation and a real watering plan. Expect losses. |
| **November – March** | Avoid | Possible with an exemption and daily attention. Expensive, high-risk, and the plants never fully catch up. |

If your schedule lands the planting in November, split the job: hard landscaping and
irrigation now, planting in April. A four-month delay costs nothing but patience.

## Sourcing: size that establishes

Choose where to buy before you choose what to buy. Greenlife Industry WA, the state
nursery and garden industry association, publishes the
list of [Waterwise Garden Centres](https://greenlifeindustrywa.com.au/waterwise-plant-directory/),
metro and regional;
the [Waterwise plants directory](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/Waterwise/Waterwise-plants)
doubles as an availability check, because it is built from what WA wholesale growers
actually produce. For natives, cross-check the species against
[Kings Park's grow-it-at-home list](https://www.bgpa.wa.gov.au/grow-it-at-home).

| Stock size | Use it for | Establishment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tubestock / forestry tube (50 mm) | Mass native planting, groundcovers, revegetation-style beds | Fastest per dollar. Looks like nothing for six months. Needs a weed-free bed. |
| 100–150 mm pot | Shrubs, strappy plants, groundcovers | The workhorse. Fast, cheap, reliable. |
| 200 mm pot | Feature shrubs, accents | Good. Sensible upper limit for most shrubs. |
| 250–300 mm / 25 L | Small trees, immediate mid-level structure | Slower. Worth it only where the design needs height now. |
| 45 L and above | Trees | Slow. Two to three summers of deliberate watering before it is self-sufficient. |
| 100 L and above | Instant specimen tree | Highest cost, highest risk, needs machinery, and frequently overtaken by a 45 L planted the same day. |

At the nursery, before anything goes in the trailer:

- **Slide one out of its pot.** The root crown should sit at the uppermost surface of
  the root ball, the root ball should be wider than it is high, and there should be
  no woody circling, kinked or J roots. Those checks come straight from
  [AS 2303](https://www.treesimpact.com.au/article/as-2303-assessing-for-conformity),
  the Australian tree stock standard.
- **Reject roots escaping the drainage holes into the nursery ground bed** — they tear
  off at loading — and anything that wobbles in its pot, which was potted up last week
  and is a small plant at a big-pot price.
- **Check the species against the [declared plant list](https://www.dpird.wa.gov.au/businesses/pests-weeds-and-diseases/plant/declared-plants/).**
  If it is declared under the Biosecurity and Agriculture Management Act 2007 you are
  legally obliged to control it, not plant it.
- **Ask whether the nursery is NIASA-accredited**, particularly if your plan contains
  banksias, grass trees or anything else in the Proteaceae. Phytophthora dieback
  arrives in potting media, and
  [more than 40% of WA native species are susceptible](https://www.dwg.org.au/about-dieback/phytophthora-dieback/).

## The hole

1. **Soak the plant in its pot** an hour before planting. A root ball that goes into
   Perth sand dry may never wet again — water moves around it, not into it.
2. **Dig wider than deep.** Two to three times the width of the root ball, and only
   as deep as the root ball. Roots spread sideways; digging deep and backfilling
   loose just means the plant sinks and the crown ends up buried.
3. **Set the root ball level with, or 10 to 20 mm proud of, finished soil level.** In
   sand, always err proud. A buried crown rots.
4. **Tease root-bound plants.** If the roots are circling the pot wall, run a clean
   knife 10 to 20 mm deep down the sides of the root ball in three or four vertical
   cuts and pull the base apart with your fingers. It feels brutal and it is correct —
   roots left circling keep circling and eventually girdle the plant. A badly
   pot-bound tree is a return, not a repair.
5. **Backfill with the improved site soil**, not a pocket of premium mix. A rich
   pocket in sand behaves like a bathtub and roots refuse to leave it. Firm with your
   hands, not your boot.
6. **No fertiliser in the hole.** Surface-applied, low-phosphorus, controlled-release
   feed after mulching, or wait six weeks. Proteaceae — banksia, grevillea, hakea —
   are damaged by ordinary phosphorus rates.
7. **Water in immediately and generously, with a wetting agent.** This collapses air
   pockets; it is not feeding.
8. **Mulch to 5 to 10 cm of coarse mulch, held clear of every stem and trunk.** For
   trees, form a shallow saucer at the root ball edge first so water goes down rather
   than sideways.

## Spacing

Space on **mature width**, not on how the bed looks on planting day. For a mass
planting that should knit closed, set plant centres at roughly the mature width; for
specimens you want to read individually, about 1.5 times mature width. The
[Water Corporation garden designs](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/garden/garden-designs-for-perth-and-the-south-west)
are the easiest free calibration — open the native plan beside yours and compare
counts per square metre.

Overplanting charges you twice: once for plants you will pull out, and again in drawn,
weak, disease-prone survivors. Accept a garden that looks sparse for one winter.

## Staking: only when needed, and loosely

Most shrubs need no stake. Stake only where the plant cannot hold itself up, the site
is genuinely exposed, or the root ball will rock in loose sand before it anchors.

When you do: two or three stakes driven into undisturbed ground **outside** the root
ball, wide flexible webbing ties in a figure-of-eight, fixed **low** on the stem so
the top third can still move. A trunk that never flexes never thickens. Remove the
stakes after twelve months — the
[international arboriculture guidance](https://www.treesaregood.org/treeowner/plantingatree)
is explicit that trees develop stronger trunks unstaked, and a forgotten tie becomes
a girdle.

**Before you plant a tree, look up.** You are responsible for keeping vegetation on
your property clear of powerlines, and if Western Power has to cut a tree you planted
or irrigated, it may
[recover $441 per span](https://www.westernpower.com.au/safety/safety-at-home/make-the-safe-call/managing-trees-near-powerlines/our-vegetation-inspection-program/).
Choose a smaller species instead.

## Establishment watering: the part that decides the budget

Apply for the [watering exemption](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/exemptions-and-breaches/watering-exemption/apply-for-a-watering-exemption)
online **on the day you plant**, because the period runs from installation, not from
approval. You get up to 42 days if it is granted between 1 October and 31 March, and
up to 35 days between 1 April and 30 September. Breaching the conditions risks a $500
fine. If you plant between 1 June and 31 August, the exemption is the only lawful way
to run sprinklers at all — the switch-off applies to bore users as well as scheme
water, with a $100 fine.

Water Corporation publishes the schedule the exemption expects. Follow it rather than
improvising:

| Period | Autumn/winter schedule (1 Apr – 30 Sep) |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–14 | 4 mm, twice a day (before 9am and about 2pm) |
| Days 15–28 | 7.5 mm, once a day, before 9am |
| Days 29–35 | 7.5 mm, every second day, before 9am |

Then the exemption ends, and this is where plant budgets die. From day 36 the garden
drops to two rostered days a week, once on each day, before 9am or after 6pm — a
severe cut, at a point when the plants are nowhere near self-sufficient.

The bridge is hand watering, which is
[permitted on any day of the week](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/Help-and-advice/Waterwise/Garden/Tips-for-hand-watering)
with a watering can or hand-held hose. Through the first summer, every tree and larger
shrub gets a deliberate deep hand watering once a week — a slow bucket at the root
ball edge, not a spray over the leaves. Two summers for anything 45 litres and up.
Ten minutes a week, and it is the difference between an established garden and a
replanting bill.

## Safety

- **Potting mix and bulk soil.** Bagged mixes and bulk supplies in WA have been linked
  to Legionnaires' disease through *Legionella longbeachae*. [WA Health](https://www.health.wa.gov.au/Articles/J_M/Legionella-control/Legionella-and-garden-soils)
  advises a face mask, gloves, avoiding dust and aerosols, and washing your hands.
  Open bags outdoors, at ground level, facing away.
- **Heat.** Do not plant on a 38-degree day. You will make poor decisions and so will
  the plants.
- **Weight.** A 100 litre tree with a wet root ball is well over 100 kg. Two people or
  a machine, and never lift a tree by its trunk.
- **Overhead and underground.** Powerlines above; services below, checked against your
  services plan before you dig a tree pit.

## What happens next

Your beds are planted, watered in and under an exemption. The next lesson lays turf,
which runs its own establishment schedule under the same exemption, and then
[mulch, edging and finishing](/curriculum/build/mulch-edging-and-finishing/) closes
the surface and locks the moisture in.
