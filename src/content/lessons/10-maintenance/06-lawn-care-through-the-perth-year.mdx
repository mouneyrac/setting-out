A Perth lawn does not die in February. It dies the previous September, when nobody cored it, nobody put wetting agent down, and the mower was set to the same height it had been set to for three years. February is just when you find out.

Everything here is a small job done at the right time. The whole discipline is the calendar, and the calendar is not the European one — the lawn's growth year runs roughly September to May, and from 1 June to 31 August your sprinklers are illegal to run at all. If you are reading this in early August you are inside the [Winter Sprinkler Switch-off](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/winter-sprinkler-switch-off), which is the right time to plan, because the renovation window opens in about four weeks.

## What a professional does at this stage

A commercial turf operator does not "look after the lawn". They run four scheduled interventions a year and mow between them.

Warm-season turf on Perth sand fails in only a few ways — it runs out of water because the soil repels it, it runs out of root depth because it is cut too short, it chokes on its own thatch, or it gets fed at the wrong time and either burns or leaches straight past the roots into the groundwater. Each of those has a fixed month attached to it. Book the interventions first and mowing becomes just mowing.

## Mowing height: the number that does the most work

Set the base height by species. These are the WA figures, from the joint Turf Growers Association and Water Corporation guide.

| Species | Summer height | Summer frequency | Winter height | Thatch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Soft leaf buffalo** | 15–20 mm | every 7–10 days | same, every 3–5 weeks | Low — do not scarify |
| **Couch** | 10–12 mm | every 10–14 days | raise to 12–16 mm | Moderate — annual dethatch |
| **Kikuyu** | 12–16 mm | every 10–14 days | raise to 16–20 mm | High — annual dethatch |
| **Zoysia** | 15–20 mm | every 14–21 days | same, every 3–5 weeks | Low — watch it |
| **Queensland Blue** | 10–13 mm | every 10–14 days | raise to 14–16 mm | Scarify every 2–3 years |

Three rules sit on top of the table.

**Never remove more than a third of the leaf in one cut.** Take more and you stall root growth and the lawn needs more water, not less, for weeks afterwards. If it has got away from you, cut it in two passes a few days apart.

**Raise the cut going into the heat.** Perth's brutal stretch is mid-December to the end of February. Move to the top of your species' band before Christmas, and add another 5 mm if the lawn browns off between waterings. More leaf shades the soil, cuts evaporation and holds the deeper root system that carries the plant through a 40-degree week. [Clemson's mowing guidance](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/mowing-lawns/) puts it plainly: hold a higher cut under heat, drought or shade stress. Drop back in March when growth picks up.

**Raise it again in winter,** for a different reason — low light and slow growth mean the plant needs leaf area to feed itself. [Water Corporation's rule](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/lawn-care/how-to-keep-your-lawn-healthy-during-winter) is not to let it go below 25 mm.

Keep the blade sharp. A blunt blade tears rather than cuts, the tips brown off within two days, and the extra wound area costs you moisture. Leave the clippings on unless the lawn is diseased; they return roughly 4% nitrogen by weight.

## Wetting agent, before the heat and not after

On Bassendean grey sand a lawn can be watered correctly and still die of thirst, because the sand is hydrophobic and the water channels past the root zone. See [Perth soils](/curriculum/site-analysis/perth-soils/) if you have not identified yours.

Apply a wetting agent three to four times a year: **early September**, straight after coring so it goes down the holes; **late November**, before the first real heat; **mid-to-late January**, when the dry patches show; and **late May**, so the soil absorbs the winter rain instead of shedding it.

Water it in on the day you apply it, on a roster day. A granular wetting agent left dry on the surface does nothing.

## Fertiliser timed to growth, not to the calendar

Perth sand holds almost no nutrient, so overfeeding does not make a greener lawn — it makes a bigger nitrate plume heading for the groundwater and eventually the river. Two credible schedules exist. [Fertilise Wise](https://www.sercul.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/FertiliseWise-BrochureALL.pdf), the Perth catchment programme, says two applications in spring and two in early autumn — September, October, November, March, April — and explicitly not in summer or winter, because summer feeding drives water use and winter feeding washes into stormwater. Water Corporation allows a light slow-release feed from early spring and every two months through the warm months. On deep sand, follow Fertilise Wise. Either way the ceilings are the same:

- Slow-release, with **no more than 1% phosphorus**
- **[No more than 2 kg per 100 m² per application](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/help-and-advice/waterwise/lawn-care/maintaining-a-healthy-lawn)** (an ice cream container full per 100 m²), which lines up with the Fertilise Wise maximum of 25 g/m² of complete fertiliser
- **12 g/m² maximum** for a nitrogen-only product above 20% N
- Water it in on a roster day, and **never** apply ahead of forecast rain

A potassium feed in autumn (sulphate of potash) toughens the lawn for winter without pushing soft growth. More on the underlying chemistry in [fertilising Perth sand](/curriculum/maintenance/fertilising-perth-sand/).

## Thatch, coring and the spring renovation

Thatch is the spongy brown layer between the green leaf and the soil. Cut a plug with a spade and look at it. [Water Corporation puts the trigger at 2.5 mm](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/Help-and-advice/Waterwise/Lawn-care/Bring-your-brown-lawn-back-to-life), which is conservative; the practical test is whether the mower rides up on it and whether water sits on top rather than soaking in.

**Kikuyu and couch build thatch and need an annual dethatch. Buffalo does not get scarified — ever.** Buffalo spreads by above-ground stolons with no rhizomes underneath, so a scarifier removes the only tissue it can regrow from and you get a dead rectangle that costs a pallet of turf to fix. Manage buffalo thatch by mowing regularly at the right height instead.

The renovation is one weekend in **September or early October**, in this order:

1. **Mow low** — a scalping cut, the one time a year the one-third rule is broken. Couch, kikuyu and Queensland Blue only.
2. **Verti-mow or scarify** if the thatch warrants it. Hire, roughly a half-day.
3. **Core** — hollow tines 50–75 mm deep, holes 50–80 mm apart, [two passes at right angles](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/aerating-lawns/). Leave the cores to break down.
4. **Wetting agent**, watered in.
5. **Top-dress** with a sand and loam blend to fill the holes and level the surface. Never a wood-chip-based product.
6. **First feed of the year.**

Do it while the grass is actively growing so the holes close over. Doing it in December turns a tired lawn into a dead one.

## Safety, and the legal edges

- **Flag every sprinkler head and valve box before you core or scarify.** A hollow tine punches straight through a 19 mm poly lateral and a scarifier shreds shallow lighting cable. This is the most common way a renovation becomes a retic repair.
- **Disconnect the spark plug lead** before you go near a mower blade or a blocked chute. Hired verti-mowers and corers are heavy, awkward, two-person lifts.
- Mow before 9am from December to February, in eye and hearing protection.
- Keep pets and children off treated lawn until the fertiliser or wetting agent is watered in and the surface has dried.
- Before running a mower or line trimmer over dry grass on the verge in summer, check whether a [Total Fire Ban](https://www.dfes.wa.gov.au/hazard-information/bushfire/total-fire-ban) is in force — DFES declares them either the afternoon before or on the day, and publishes the declared bans on Emergency WA.

## Watering the thing

Through the growing season the target is **10 mm twice a week**, on your two rostered days, before 9am or after 6pm. On sand that usually means splitting the run — two shorter cycles half an hour apart deliver more to the roots than one long one that runs off. Catch-cup it rather than guessing; the method and the run times per sprinkler type are in [watering and the retic audit](/curriculum/maintenance/watering-and-retic-audit/).

Put the four interventions into your six-season calendar in [the Perth year](/curriculum/maintenance/the-perth-year/), and if you re-turf a patch during the renovation, apply for the watering exemption the day you lay it.
