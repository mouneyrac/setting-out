Perth's watering rules sit in the approvals phase for the same reason setbacks and
pool fencing do. They decide what you are allowed to build, they are free to design
around now, and they are expensive to discover in November with a dead lawn and an
infringement notice on the bench.

The short version: two watering days a week, fixed by your house number. On each of
those days you may water once, before 9am or after 6pm. From 1 June to 31 August you
may not use sprinklers at all. None of it is negotiable, and none of it cares whether
the water comes from a Water Corporation meter or a bore you paid for yourself.

Everything below was checked against Water Corporation and wa.gov.au on 2 August 2026.
The framework dates from the Permanent Water Efficiency Measures introduced statewide
in 2007, and the roster and switch-off are unchanged as at today.

## What a professional does at this stage

A landscaper who has been through a few Perth summers designs backwards from the
roster rather than forwards from the plan. Four things happen before a concept drawing
exists.

The two days and the water source get written down, because a garden that needs three
waterings a week in February is an illegal garden, not a thirsty one. Turf area and
plant palette get sized to what two waterings can sustain on sand — see
[hydrozoning](/curriculum/concept-design/hydrozoning/). The **planting date is chosen
deliberately**, because the new-garden exemption is the only legal way to water a new
lawn daily and it is anchored to the day of installation. And if a bore is in play, its
viability and licensing get settled before anything is drawn that assumes free water.

The amateur version is to build the garden, then find out. That is how people end up
laying instant turf on 2 June.

## The roster

The Department of Water and Environmental Regulation publishes the
[full table](https://www.wa.gov.au/service/building-utilities-and-essential-services/water-supply/check-garden-bore-water-restrictions).
For Perth and Mandurah, from 1 September to 31 May, your two days are set by the last
digit of your house number — and scheme users and garden bore users get identical days.

| Last digit of house number | Your two watering days |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Wednesday and Saturday |
| 2 | Thursday and Sunday |
| 3 | Friday and Monday |
| 4 | Saturday and Tuesday |
| 5 | Sunday and Wednesday |
| 6 | Monday and Thursday |
| 7 | Tuesday and Friday |
| 8 | Wednesday and Saturday |
| 9 | Thursday and Sunday |
| 0 | Friday and Monday |

Three things this table does not tell you:

- **It is your street number, not your unit number.** Every villa or apartment behind
  number 42 waters on number 42's days.
- **Confirm it with the official lookup anyway.** [Check your watering days](https://www.watercorporation.com.au/waterwise/watering-days/check-your-watering-days)
  also tells you whether your address is inside the Perth and Mandurah area at all.
  Outside it the rules differ — Albany and the northern and southern country areas
  allow any day, once a day, in the same time windows.
- **If two properties share one bore** and cannot isolate the reticulation, each
  property may only irrigate on the days that apply to the property where the bore is
  located. Worth knowing before you agree to share one with a neighbour.

## The windows, and what a breach costs

Once a day, **before 9am or after 6pm**, on your rostered days. Not both windows.
Not twice.

The fine is $100, issued on the spot. Inspectors work seven days a week, every
complaint is investigated, and Water Corporation's published position is blunt: a
controller malfunction will not save you. That matters on a renovation, because a
controller still running the previous owner's programme is running someone else's
roster. Reprogram it the week you take possession, not the week you start work.

## 1 June to 31 August: the switch-off

No sprinklers at all, anywhere in Perth, Mandurah and parts of the South West and
Great Southern. It is mandatory for households and businesses alike, and it binds
licensed groundwater users too — councils, schools, golf courses.

The one real exception is supply-based: the switch-off does not apply to irrigation
systems fed from a rainwater tank. If your design includes a tank, that is a genuine
legal advantage worth designing for — see
[irrigation water source](/curriculum/build/irrigation-water-source/). For everyone
else, winter planting happens under an exemption or not at all.

## Bores are not a loophole

This is the most common misunderstanding on a Perth block, and it costs people money
every winter.

A garden bore in Perth or Mandurah is on **the same roster, the same time windows and
the same three-month switch-off** as scheme water. Water Corporation inspectors who
see a bore user watering during the switch-off pass the breach to the Department of
Water and Environmental Regulation. Owning the pump changes your water bill; it does
not change your obligations.

It also does not change the water. Perth bore water is frequently high in iron, which
stains limestone, render and paving orange and progressively blocks drip emitters. The
department's fix is preventive: correct sprinkler selection and no overspray onto hard
surfaces — a design decision now, not a stain you scrub later. And **garden bore water
is not safe to drink**: not for people, not for a wading pool, not for washing produce
you intend to eat raw.

## Does your bore need a licence?

Most suburban bores do not, but check rather than assume. Under the department's
[garden bore regulation](https://begroundwaterwise.wa.gov.au/garden-bore-regulation/)
page, you need a water licence in a proclaimed groundwater area if you irrigate
**0.2 hectares (2,000 square metres) or more**, use the bore commercially, or draw
from an artesian aquifer — in Perth, the Leederville or Yarragadee, not the shallow
superficial aquifer most garden bores tap. The threshold rarely bites on a standard
suburban lot. It does bite on a rural-residential block, and whenever the irrigation
earns money.

## If you are drilling a new bore

Run the [Perth Groundwater Map](https://maps.water.wa.gov.au/#/webmap/gwm) first. It
gives depth to watertable, expected salinity, and the depth to the base of the
superficial aquifer — the maximum depth you may drill for non-commercial use without a
groundwater licence. If the water under you is saline, a bore is a way to kill a garden
slowly rather than water it.

Then use an accredited driller. The department recommends drillers accredited by the
Australian Drilling Industry Association, construction to the **Minimum Construction
Requirements for Water Bores in Australia, 4th Edition 2020**, and a written bore log
recording depth, soil composition, yield, and pump make and model. Keep that log.

This is not DIY work. Drilling means rotating machinery and an open hole, and an
uncapped or poorly sealed bore is both a physical hazard and a contamination path
straight into the aquifer. Wiring the pump is electrical work, which in WA is a
licensed trade — see
[irrigation controllers and compliance](/curriculum/build/irrigation-controllers-and-compliance/).
Ring your council before you book a driller: some require approval for a bore or its
pump enclosure, and pump noise is a routine source of neighbour complaints.

## The new-garden exemption

This is the rule that decides your build programme, so read it before you set a planting
date. For a new lawn or garden — including roll-on lawn, runners, shredded lawn and
seed — you can apply to Water Corporation for a watering exemption that lets you water
outside the roster, including during the switch-off.

| Installed between | Exemption length | Recommended water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 October and 31 March | Up to 42 days from planting | 10 mm a day |
| 1 April and 30 September | Up to 35 days from planting | 7.5 mm a day |

Four things to get right:

1. **Apply on the day the lawn or garden goes in.** The clock starts at planting, so
   applying late shortens your own exemption.
2. **You pay for the water.** It counts towards your billing year usage.
3. **The conditions bind you, not your contractor.** A breach is the property owner's
   responsibility, not the turf supplier's or the retic installer's, and
   non-compliance risks a **$500 fine** — five times the ordinary roster fine.
4. **Normal roster resumes the day the exemption ends.** Plan the handover so the turf
   is established enough to survive two days a week. See
   [turf for Perth](/curriculum/build/turf-for-perth/).

## Commissioning and testing, legally

You may run each station for **no more than two minutes**, on your rostered days,
before 9am or after 6pm — enough to check coverage station by station and nothing more.
There is no exemption for bore owners testing their own system.

A Waterwise specialist is exempt from watering schedules, including during the
switch-off, which is the only lawful way to commission or audit a whole system in a
single winter visit. If your build finishes in June, book one.

## What this changes downstream

Two watering days and a three-month ban is the hardest constraint in the course. It
caps lawn area, pushes the plant palette toward things that survive without weekly
water, sets station run times, and fixes your planting date. Carry your two days, your
windows, your source and your exemption plan into
[hydrozoning](/curriculum/concept-design/hydrozoning/) and
[irrigation scheduling and uniformity](/curriculum/build/irrigation-scheduling-and-uniformity/).
