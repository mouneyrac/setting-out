A pool barrier is not a fence. It is life-safety law, and you are personally on the hook for it
every day the pool holds more than 300 mm of water — not just on the day it was signed off.
Under [regulation 50(2) of the Building Regulations 2012](https://www.legislation.wa.gov.au/legislation/statutes.nsf/law_s43955.html),
each **owner and occupier** must ensure a compliant barrier is in place, with a maximum fine of
$5,000 and a $1,000 infringement notice for getting it wrong.

This lesson sits before concept design for one reason: the barrier constrains the garden far
more than the garden constrains the barrier. Levels, paving, retaining, planting, the barbecue,
the pot beside the pump — all of it is subordinate to clearances that do not bend. Landscapers
break compliance on established pools constantly, almost always by raising a level or building
something within half a metre of the fence.

If you are renovating around an existing pool, assume nothing. There can legally be four years
between council inspections, so a certificate from 2023 tells you very little about today.

## What a professional does at this stage

Four things, before a single line is drawn.

1. **Establish the pool's age bracket.** Pre-November 2001, pre-May 2016, or later. This decides
   which standard the barrier may comply with and which concessions exist. If you cannot date it,
   ask the council.
2. **Identify every element that forms the barrier today.** Rarely just the pool fence — a
   boundary fence, house wall, window, retaining wall, above-ground pool side wall or shed wall
   can all be part of it.
3. **Get the last inspection result.** The council provides the most recent inspection
   certificate on request to the owner. Find yours through the
   [WALGA local government directory](https://www.walga.asn.au/about-local-government/online-local-government-directory.aspx).
4. **Draw the exclusion envelope on the plan first.** Hatch the 500 mm strip and the
   non-climbable zones on both sides, then design outside them. That is the whole discipline.

## The trigger, and what counts

Water more than 300 mm deep — in-ground, above-ground, spa, portable, temporary, or a toddler's
wading pool. A spa bath in a bathroom is not caught, and nor is a fish pond, though a pond still
drowns children. A lockable spa cover is **not** a barrier: when the cover is off there is no
barrier at all. An empty pool needs no barrier but stays on the council register, and rain or a
leaking retic solenoid refills one faster than you would think.

## The non-climbable zone in plain terms

A barrier must be at least **1.2 m high measured on the outside**, permanently fixed, vertical
(it may lean away from the pool by up to 15 degrees but never towards it), with gaps between
vertical members no more than 100 mm and no more than 100 mm between the barrier and a *stable*
ground surface beneath it. Sand is not a stable surface. Pavers, concrete and decking are.

Building and Energy's [Rules for Pools and Spas](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/publications/rules-pools-and-spas)
sets out five non-climbable zones. They apply to any barrier under 1.8 m high, and always to a
boundary barrier.

| Zone | Where it is | What it means on site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NCZ 1 | A 900 mm span anywhere on the face of the barrier | No handhold or foothold pair closer than 900 mm apart |
| NCZ 2 | A 900 mm quadrant sweeping down and away from the barrier, outside the pool area | Nothing climbable in that arc — this is the one gardens breach |
| NCZ 3 | A 900 mm quadrant from the top of the barrier, up and away | Overhanging branches, pergola beams, screen tops |
| NCZ 4 | 900 mm high by 300 mm deep on the *poolside* | Only required where gaps between vertical members exceed 10 mm |
| NCZ 5 | A 900 mm quadrant from the top of a boundary fence, on the poolside | Only for boundary barriers using the 1.8 m poolside option |

Two more rules that do most of the damage to garden layouts:

- **Nothing within 500 mm.** No object, step, retaining wall or ground level change that reduces
  the barrier's required height may sit within 500 mm of it.
- **Projections over 10 mm count** as potential footholds unless chamfered at 60 degrees flush to
  the face behind. That includes gate hinges — see
  [Industry Bulletin 123](https://www.wa.gov.au/media/53576/download?inline), which tells
  installers to space the bottom of the top hinge 900 mm clear of the top of the bottom hinge.

## The garden decisions this kills

| What you wanted | Why it fails | What to do instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raised bed or low retaining wall against the fence | Level change inside 500 mm, reduces effective height | Hold the bed 600 mm off and drop the wall below fence level |
| Lifting the paving 100 mm to fix a fall | Reduces barrier height on that side | Regrade away from the barrier, or raise the fence to suit |
| Bench, pot, barbecue or filter box near the fence | Climbable object in NCZ 2 | Everything frequently used belongs outside the pool area |
| Tree with a low limb over the fence | Handhold in NCZ 3 | Choose clear-trunk species; write the pruning into maintenance |
| Trellis or screen fixed to the boundary fence | Creates footholds inside NCZ 5 | Freestanding screen set outside the zone |
| Deck stepping up towards the barrier | Level change inside 500 mm | Terminate the deck 600 mm short with a flush edge |

A pergola beside a pool has to satisfy both these rules and
[structures and building permits](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/); a new
wall has to satisfy [retaining walls approval](/curriculum/approvals/retaining-walls-approval/)
as well.

## Planting near the barrier

Vegetation near a barrier must be maintained so it does not affect compliance. Plants that do not
act as a handhold or foothold are unlikely to be treated as facilitating climbing and are
typically allowed inside a non-climbable zone; plants that cannot support the weight of a young
child are typically permitted within 500 mm.

As a plant list: soft, non-woody, low-forking, nothing that scrambles up the fence. Dietes,
lomandra, agapanthus, low grevilleas and clumping grasses are fine. A frangipani, a lilly pilly
hedge, a bougainvillea on the fence and anything with a fork at 400 mm are not. Write the pruning
into your maintenance schedule — compliance is judged on inspection day, not planting day. See
[the Perth year](/curriculum/maintenance/the-perth-year/).

## Gates: where the failures actually are

Building and Energy states that **90 per cent of young children who drowned in WA private pools
reached the water through the gate area**, and gates are the most common non-compliance found by
inspectors. Treat the gate as the design problem, not an afterthought.

- Opens **outwards**, away from the pool. Never inwards, never sliding, never into a doorway.
- Self-closes from **any** open position, including resting on the latch, without bouncing open.
- Self-latches, and cannot re-open without operating the latch.
- Latch release at least 1.5 m above ground on the outside — or on the poolside with a 450 mm
  shield, at least 150 mm below the top of the gate, and no gap over 10 mm.
- No more than 100 mm under the gate, over a stable surface. Grass under a pool gate grows and
  stops it closing.
- No pet doors, ever.

Keep gates out of the wind, and use a single shared footing for both posts so they cannot move
independently and throw the latch out of alignment. Keeping an existing frameless glass fence?
Read the [gate latch warning](https://www.wa.gov.au/government/announcements/non-compliant-swimming-pool-gate-latch-assemblies)
first.

## Using the boundary fence

Common in Perth, and the source of most neighbour disputes. Two options:

| Option | Requirement | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Poolside** | At least 1.8 m high on your side, with a 900 mm NCZ 5 down from the top on the poolside | Almost always — compliance stays within your control |
| **Non-poolside** | At least 1.2 m on the neighbour's side, nothing within 500 mm reducing that height, NCZ 1, 2, 3 and 4 satisfied on their side | Only where the fence abuts a verge, park or public land |

The non-poolside option puts your legal compliance in your neighbour's hands: if they lean a
trailer on the fence, you are non-compliant. A barrier set back 900 mm from the boundary may be
treated as an internal barrier instead, which is often the cheaper answer.

## Permits, inspections and money

A building permit must be in effect **before** the pool is constructed or installed. Since
21 June 2024 most pool fences do not need one, unless the fence also acts as a required fall
protection barrier, is masonry over 0.75 m, is non-masonry over 1.8 m, or is in cyclonic wind
region C or D. From 1 July 2026 the permit fee is 0.19 per cent of the estimated value of the
work certified, or 0.32 per cent uncertified, minimum $121.

The builder named on the permit lodges a BA7 notice of completion within seven days. The council
must then inspect within 30 days, re-inspect within 60 days if it fails, and inspect at least
every four years thereafter. Councils may charge up to $312 for the initial inspection and its
re-inspections, and up to $78 a year for the periodic program — payable whether or not you are
inspected that year.

One recent change matters if a supplier quotes you a standard. BCA 2025 became mandatory for new
private pools on **1 May 2026** and it references AS 1926.1:2024 — but Western Australia has not
adopted that edition for private pools. **AS 1926.1-2012 still applies here**, as modified by
regulation 15B. If a quote cites the 2024 standard, the supplier is working to the wrong
document.

## The hazards, named

- **Never take a barrier down "just for the day".** If work requires removing a panel, wall or
  gate, an alternative compliant barrier must restrict access for the entire period it is gone.
  A leaning mesh panel and a witches hat is not that.
- **Electrical work near water is a licensed trade.** Metal fencing, gates, handrails and light
  fittings within 1.25 m of the pool edge must be bonded to the main earthing system by a
  licensed electrical contractor. Replacing a metal pool fence is not purely a fencing job.
- **Silica dust.** Cutting limestone block, pavers or concrete needs on-tool water suppression
  and respiratory protection — see
  [site establishment and safety](/curriculum/build/site-establishment-and-safety/).
- **Excavating beside a pool shell** can crack it and undermine the barrier's footings. Both are
  your cost.

## What this changes downstream

Your plan now carries a hatched exclusion envelope: 500 mm clear of the barrier, plus the
non-climbable arcs above, below and behind it.
[Functional zoning](/curriculum/concept-design/functional-zoning/) and
[levels and grading](/curriculum/documentation/levels-and-grading-plan/) both have to respect it.
