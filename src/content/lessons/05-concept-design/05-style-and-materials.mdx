A material palette is not a mood board. A mood board is a picture of a garden you
liked. A palette is a short list of materials you have agreed to use and, more
usefully, a much longer list you have agreed not to. Its job is to make every
later decision faster and cheaper, because by the time you are standing in a
supply yard at 7am you should already know exactly what you are buying.

On a renovation the palette starts with materials you did not choose. The house
has a brick colour and a roof colour. There is probably a limestone wall, some
grey concrete pavers and a jarrah gate a previous owner put in. Those are in the
palette whether you like it or not. Your real decision is whether the new work
agrees with them or deliberately replaces them — because "nearly matches" is the
one answer that always looks wrong.

## What a professional does at this stage

Three things, in this order.

**They shorten the list.** A residential garden that reads as designed usually
runs on about five materials: one paving, one walling, one timber, one metal, one
mulch. Everything else is a repeat of one of those. Amateur gardens are rarely
under-designed; they are over-supplied. Four paving types, three edge treatments,
two fence colours.

**They test the material against the environment before the eye.** Salt, sun,
sand, bore water and pool chemistry each destroy materials that looked perfect on
a screen. This is where the money is lost, and it stays invisible for two summers.

**They check what the material drags in legally.** Some materials trigger a
building permit, a bushfire construction standard, a verge approval or a licensed
trade. That check happens before the material enters the palette, not after the
quote lands.

Then they write it down as a specification with a rate against every line.

## The WA palette, material by material

| Material | How it ages in Perth | The mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Natural limestone block** | Softens, spalls and rounds off. Greens on the shaded south face. Erodes visibly where a sprinkler hits it | Watering it. And cleaning it with brick acid — limestone is calcium carbonate and acid dissolves it |
| **Reconstituted limestone block** | Consistent, cement-bound, holds its arris. Fades slightly, stains readily | Assuming a keyed block means no engineering is needed |
| **Liquid limestone** | Pale, stays cooler barefoot than dark surfaces. Porous, so it stains. Fine crazing is normal | Treating it as repairable. It cannot be lifted and relaid |
| **Concrete pavers** | Very durable. Colour settles in 2–3 summers, efflorescence blooms then clears | Choosing the colour from a dry sample in a showroom |
| **Granite and dense natural stone** | Effectively permanent, does not absorb bore iron | Dark stone on a west-facing terrace. It is unusable at 5pm in February |
| **Jarrah** | Class 2 durable, 15–40 years above ground. Silvers within 18 months if not oiled | Oiling it once and calling it done, or using sapwood-bearing boards |
| **Marri** | Moderately durable above ground (7–15 years) and in ground (5–15 years) | Using a beautiful local furniture and flooring timber as decking or posts |
| **Composite decking** | No splinters, no oiling. Colour fades and settles early, dark boards get very hot | Ignoring thermal movement and the tighter joist spacing |
| **Corten / weathering steel** | Patina stabilises only where it wets and dries. Runoff stains everything below it | Putting it above pale paving, or within a few hundred metres of the surf |
| **Rammed earth** | Excellent, if capped and detailed. Erodes from the top down if not | Treating it as a garden wall instead of a structure |

### Limestone: know which one you are buying

Two different products share the name. **Natural limestone block** is quarried
rock — variable in density, warm in colour, soft enough to carve with a hand saw,
and honest about its age within five years. **Reconstituted limestone block** is a
manufactured cement product with limestone aggregate: consistent, stronger, and
available in keyed and engineered profiles. Midland Brick publishes prices, and
on 2 August 2026 its
[Blokstone limestone blocks](https://www.midlandbrick.com.au/category/blocks/limestone-blocks)
listed at $403.52 per pallet for split blocks, $721.86 for metre blocks and
$918.15 for key blocks, supply only.

Two Perth-specific warnings. Never run irrigation onto a limestone wall — it
erodes the face and, on a bore, leaves a permanent orange iron stain. And never
let anyone acid-wash it.

Any wall that retains a change in level is a structure with its own approval
pathway. That is [retaining walls approval](/curriculum/approvals/retaining-walls-approval/),
not a material choice.

### Liquid limestone and poured concrete

Crushed limestone aggregate with an off-white cement, poured, screeded and
saw-cut into panels. It is popular here for a good reason: it is pale, so it
stays far cooler underfoot than dark pavers or dark stone, which matters
enormously around a pool.

The trade-off is that it is porous and permanent. It stains from leaf tannin and
bore water unless it is sealed and re-sealed. Fine crazing is cosmetic; a crack
with a level change across it is structural — and there is no lift-and-relay,
only demolition. Poured surfaces are a one-shot decision, which is why the joint
layout belongs in your documentation rather than in the concreter's head. See
[concrete and liquid limestone](/curriculum/build/concrete-and-liquid-limestone/).

**Safety, plainly:** cutting control joints, edges and pavers generates
respirable crystalline silica. WorkSafe WA lists paver cutting and laying, and
brick, concrete and stone cutting, as silica-generating processes requiring wet
cutting or on-tool extraction plus respiratory protection. Dry-cutting limestone
or concrete in a suburban backyard with no water and no mask is the most
dangerous thing most home landscapers do, and the damage is permanent.

### Timber: jarrah, marri and the rules that catch people

Jarrah is Class 2 durable — 15 to 40 years above ground — and
[WoodSolutions](https://www.woodsolutions.com.au/wood-species/hardwood/jarrah)
lists it as acceptable for all AS 3959 applications at BAL 12.5 and BAL 19, and
for decking at BAL 29 with non-combustible wall cladding and an enclosed subfloor.
It comes only from WA native forest with no plantation supply, so price and
availability move.

Marri is milled here and is beautiful, but
[WoodSolutions](https://www.woodsolutions.com.au/wood-species/hardwood/marri)
rates it only moderately durable both above ground (7 to 15 years) and in the
ground (5 to 15 years) — Class 3 either way, against jarrah's Class 2. It is a
furniture and flooring timber, not a deck and definitely not a post.

Three rules from the durability standard that quietly ruin decks:

- A deck with **less than 400 mm clearance** needs Class 1 timber or H4
  treatment, not Class 2.
- Anything **in the ground** — posts, stumps, sleepers — needs Class 1 or H5.
- Durability applies to **heartwood only**. Sapwood is non-durable in every
  species, including jarrah.

Then decide the finish and write it down: oiled and recoated annually, or allowed
to silver. Both are legitimate. Choosing neither is how a deck ends up patchy.

### Corten and the coastal metal question

Weathering steel needs a wet-dry cycle to form and stabilise its patina. Under a
sprinkler, in permanent shade, or buried in damp sand in a planter, it does not
stabilise — it just keeps corroding. And the runoff stains: pale limestone,
liquid limestone and cream pavers under a corten screen will go orange in one wet
season. Detail a gravel drip strip, or do not put it above a pale surface.

Distance from the surf decides the metal.
[AS 4312 corrosivity categories](https://gaa.com.au/performance-in-various-environments/)
put C5 within 200 metres of rough seas and surf beaches, C4 from 200 metres to
1 kilometre, and C3 from 1 to 10 kilometres inland — and even hot dip galvanized
steel only lasts 6 to 13 years at C5. In Trigg, Scarborough, City Beach or
Cottesloe, corten is a maintenance item rather than a finish, and 316 marine-grade
stainless, aluminium or powder-coated hot dip galvanized steel are the honest
choices. Ten kilometres inland in Bassendean, none of this applies.

### Rammed earth

Western Australia builds more stabilised rammed earth than anywhere else in the
country. Cement-stabilised soil compacted in formwork, typically around 300 mm
thick, with real mass and real thermal lag.

Two things people get wrong. It is a structure, not a garden wall: it needs a
footing, and above a modest height it needs engineering. And the top must be
capped or protected, because unprotected rammed earth erodes from the top down.
[HB 195](https://www.intertekinform.com/en-au/standards/hb-195-2002-131125_saig_as_as_277783/)
is what your builder and engineer will work from.

## Four checks before anything enters the palette

1. **Distance to the surf** decides your metals and your fixings.
2. **Bushfire prone area.** Check your address on the
   [DFES map](https://www.dfes.wa.gov.au/hazard-information/bushfire/bushfire-prone-areas).
   If your lot is designated, decks and Class 10a structures attached to the house
   must meet AS 3959 at your assessed BAL, and the current Map of Bush Fire Prone
   Areas took effect on 13 December 2025 — so a block that was clear a few years
   ago may not be now. See [structures and building permits](/curriculum/approvals/structures-and-building-permits/).
3. **Water source.** Bore water carries iron and stains limestone and pale
   concrete orange, permanently. See
   [irrigation water source](/curriculum/build/irrigation-water-source/).
4. **Pool and aspect.** Salt and chlorinated water on limestone is a slow,
   expensive argument you will lose, and dark surfaces belong in shade. See
   [designing for a Perth summer](/curriculum/concept-design/designing-for-a-perth-summer/).

One more: if any part of your palette lands on the **verge**, check your local
government's verge treatment policy first. The rules vary by council. The City of
Stirling, for example, lets you put lawn, plants or organic mulch on the verge
without approval but
[requires an approved verge treatment application before paving, synthetic turf
or in-situ concrete goes in](https://www.stirling.wa.gov.au/residential-services/services-in-your-street/streets,-verges-and-traffic).
How loose materials such as stone, gravel and crushed rock are treated varies
from council to council, so read your own council's policy rather than assuming.

## Making the palette actually constrain

Write it as a table, one row per element — paving, walling, decking, edging,
mulch, screening, furniture — with the material, a supply rate, the source of
that rate, and one line on how it ages here. Then apply the rule that makes it a
palette rather than a wish list: **a new material may only enter by displacing an
existing one.** Adding is not permitted.

Get rates from published lists where they exist — Midland Brick's paver pages
listed $73.66 to $106.56 per square metre supply only on 2 August 2026, a usable
benchmark for concrete paving. Anything unusual needs two supply quotes.
Installed rates add base, bedding, cutting, waste and labour, and
[quantity take-offs](/curriculum/costing/quantity-take-offs/) turns them into a
budget in Phase 7.

Last, hold the palette up against the house: brick colour, roof colour, window
frames, gutter colour. Match deliberately or contrast deliberately. Whatever you
kept in the [existing garden audit](/curriculum/site-analysis/auditing-an-existing-garden/)
is already a palette entry, and pretending otherwise is how renovations end up
looking like two gardens.

hydrozones, so the irrigation you eventually design has something coherent to
water.
